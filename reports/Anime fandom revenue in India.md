# India's Anime Boom Outruns Japan's Licensing Machine

The evidence supports the Torii pitch on its direction and its mechanism, though not on precise Indian revenue figures. Japan's anime industry now earns most of its money abroad: in 2024 **overseas revenue was ¥2.17 trillion, or 56.5% of a record ¥3.84 trillion market**, growing 26% against 2.8% at home, and Tokyo's ¥20 trillion content-export target for 2033 is 3.3 times the 2024 level. India is the obvious next market, ranking third in the world for anime viewing penetration at 41%, with *Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle* taking nearly seven times the previous anime box-office record. Yet Indian fans barely pay: Crunchyroll charges ₹79 a month, its Indian app had about 46,000 monthly users in 2024, and the typical fan spends ₹500–2,000 on anime every six months. Official merchandise reaches them through a few master licensees and import channels that add 40–45% to prices, while counterfeits flourish where anime merch sells (apparel, online, social-media ads), and Japan's METI now puts worldwide damage from counterfeit Japanese character goods at ¥4.7 trillion a year. The root cause is that anime licensing, with its minimum guarantees, multi-stage approvals and production-committee consensus, is built for big licensees, so a 200–500 unit creator drop has no legal path between fan-work rules and a full licence. Each Torii component copies a mechanism that already works: pay-first production is normal in Japan (Makuake handles ¥17.2B a year, Premium Bandai has 5.55M members), all-or-nothing funding has raised $8.7B on Kickstarter, and India's UPI (₹592 average merchant payment) and print-on-demand (₹177 tees) make small batches cheap. The weak spots are real: every Indian market-size figure is an analyst estimate that disagrees with the others by 2x or more, the 180 million fan figure is Crunchyroll's own claim, no anime-specific counterfeit share exists for India, and nothing yet shows that Japanese licensors will approve creator designs quickly or accept royalty-only terms.

## Japan now earns most anime revenue abroad and needs 3.3 times more

Japan's anime business already earns more abroad than at home. The Association of Japanese Animations (AJA) puts the 2024 wide-definition market at a record **¥3.84 trillion (about US$25 billion), up 14.8%**. Of that, **overseas revenue was ¥2.17 trillion (+26%) and domestic revenue was ¥1.67 trillion (+2.8%)** ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)). Overseas grew from 51.5% of the market in 2023 ([anime.eiga.com](https://anime.eiga.com/news/123132/)) to **56.5% in 2024**. By our arithmetic on AJA's figures, overseas supplied **about 91% of all 2024 growth** (¥448B of ¥494B). Two further details sharpen the pitch. First, the studios themselves earn only about a quarter of their revenue overseas (¥118.8B of ¥466.2B). Most overseas value therefore goes to licensors, platforms and committee members ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)). Second, merchandising is the largest domestic segment: **¥700.8B in 2023, about 21% of the whole market and 43% of domestic revenue**, nearly three times streaming ([anime.eiga.com](https://anime.eiga.com/news/123132/)). Merchandise licensing is how anime makes money in Japan, and it is the lever least developed in markets like India.

Policy has turned this into a national target. The revised Cool Japan Strategy (June 2024) aims for **¥20 trillion in overseas content sales by 2033**, up from ¥4.7 trillion in 2022, and calls stronger anti-piracy measures essential to getting there ([AFPBB](https://www.afpbb.com/articles/-/3522932)). Overseas content sales were about ¥5.8 trillion in 2023, more than Japan's semiconductor exports ([Adnkronos/Jiji](https://english.adnkronos.com/2026/01/13/tokyo-report-japan-to-boost-aid-for-games-anime-as-new-pillar-of-growth/)). They reached **¥6.04 trillion in 2024, which makes the 2033 goal 3.3 times the 2024 level**, with an interim milestone of ¥10 trillion in 2028 ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)). Reaching it needs about **14% compound growth a year** (our calculation). Human Media estimates that total overseas content sales grew only about 4% in 2024. Anime was about ¥2.2 trillion of the roughly ¥6 trillion, and only about ¥3.4 trillion of the total is estimated to flow back to Japan ([GameBusiness.jp](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/12/01/25630.html)). On those numbers, the target cannot be met by selling more in the US and China alone. It needs new territories and new revenue lines. Public money is modest: ¥25.3 billion of content support, against about ¥76 billion in South Korea, plus ¥35 billion in the FY2025 supplementary budget ([Adnkronos/Jiji](https://english.adnkronos.com/2026/01/13/tokyo-report-japan-to-boost-aid-for-games-anime-as-new-pillar-of-growth/)).

India is moving up Japan's priority list, but no Japanese source publishes India's share of anime revenue; AJA's public summaries do not break it out. Toei Animation's ten-year plan names India a primary focus alongside North America, China and Europe, with about ¥19 billion set aside for logistics, e-commerce, stores and events ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/toei-animation-targets-india-as-key-market-in-10-year-global-plan)). Bandai Namco India's COO said in 2023 that apparel would follow if demand justified it ([AnimeHunch](https://animehunch.com/bandai-namco-india-says-we-are-going-to-provide-products-for-all-anime-fans/)). By 2026 the company was reportedly preparing made-in-India One Piece, Dragon Ball and Naruto apparel, according to unnamed sources ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/bandai-namco-made-in-india-anime-merchandise)).

Government channels are converging on India too. dentsu noted in 2024 that IP holders had rarely taken part in Indian anime events ([dentsu](https://www.group.dentsu.com/jp/news/release/pdf-cms/2024073-1211.pdf)). The JETRO-supported Mela! Mela! Anime Japan!! in Delhi then drew **47,200 visitors with 16 IP holders in 2024** ([dentsu](https://www.group.dentsu.com/en/news/release/001386.html)) and **over 60,000 with nearly 20 IP holders in 2025** ([Celsys](https://www.celsys.com/en/topic/20251017)). The two governments have designated 2027 the India–Japan Year of Shared Horizons, with an IMAGE-75 partnership for manga, anime and gaming ([NewKerala/IANS](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/2027-celebrated-as-india-japan-year-shared-horizons-314.htm)). JETRO's January 2026 report on Indian animation focuses on Indian studios and co-production, and lists YouTube piracy and dubbing quality among the barriers ([JETRO](https://www.jetro.go.jp/biz/areareports/2026/f5f42c2bc162035c.html)). It does not cover earning from existing Japanese IP through local merchandise, which is the gap Torii targets.

## India is a top-three anime audience that pays like a fringe market

### 41% penetration and a sevenfold box-office jump

The best independent audience measure is GEM Partners' Anime Global White Paper 2026. It surveyed 15,000 people in 15 countries and ranks **India third in anime viewing penetration at 41%**, behind Japan (over 55%) and China (about 42%). Indian penetration rose more than 30% from 2020 to 2025 ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/india-ranks-among-top-three-global-anime-viewership-markets)). Crunchyroll claims about **180 million Indian anime fans** and says dubbed titles make up over 65% of its Indian viewing ([Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/brandlicense/archives/interviews/anime-alchemy-crunchyrolls-epic-saga-india.i2912)). It has also called India its number-two market by viewership and forecast that India would supply 60% of its viewership growth ([t2ONLINE](https://t2online.in/screen/streaming/global-anime-brand-crunchyroll-sees-india-as-the-second-largest-anime-market-in-the-world-after-the-us/508339)). These are company claims with no published method.

The fandom is young and watches mostly in Indian languages. A Crunchyroll-commissioned study across seven markets including India found that **54% of Gen Z identify as anime fans**, ahead of K-drama and Bollywood fandom in that age group. This is a figure for the whole sample, not for India alone ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interest/2025-05-23/crunchyroll-research-over-half-of-gen-z-globally-are-anime-fans/.224732)). Hakuhodo India's eight-city 2026 study found that **61.1% prefer dubbed anime, and 73.1% of those prefer Hindi**. It also found that 95.9% watch on smartphones ([MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/?p=2512580)).

Box-office results are the strongest evidence of behaviour, because they record money spent rather than attitudes. *Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle* opened in September 2025 on about 1,700 screens in five languages. It netted **about ₹13 crore on day one**, more than every Indian film released that Friday ([The Hollywood Reporter India](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/features/insight/demon-slayer-infinity-castle-box-office-japanese-film-records-biggest-day-1-for-anime-in-india)), and finished at **over ₹66 crore net** ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/chainsaw-man-has-modest-indian-debut-amid-demon-slayer-craze)). The previous record was *Suzume*, at **₹10 crore** gross in 2023 ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-guides/top-5-highest-grossing-anime-films-in-india-so-far)). *Jujutsu Kaisen 0* took about ₹4.85 crore net in 2022 ([Sacnilk](https://www.sacnilk.com/quicknews/Jujutsu_Kaisen_0_2022_Box_Office_Collection_Day_Wise_Worldwide)). Infinity Castle's first day beat the entire run of the previous record holder. The same data carries a warning. *Chainsaw Man – Reze Arc*, released two weeks later, made only about ₹5.4 crore over 17 days ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/chainsaw-man-reze-arc-makes-modest-gains-in-india)), so mass demand concentrates on a few franchises.

Live events show the same rising curve. Bengaluru's Japan Habba grew from about 6,300 attendees in 2019 ([Consulate-General of Japan](https://www.bengaluru.in.emb-japan.go.jp/itpr_en/00_000140.html)) to a sold-out 15,000+ in 2025 ([Japan Habba](https://japanhabba.org/)). Delhi Comic Con 2025 reported 52,000+ visitors ([IT Voice](https://www.itvoice.in/delhi-comic-con-2025-wraps-up-over-52000-footfall-over-three-days-of-unmatched-fandom-creativity-and-celebration-of-pop-culture)). Both are organiser counts.

### ₹79 subscriptions and ₹500–2,000 fan budgets

Next to that audience, measured spending is tiny. Crunchyroll moved India to **₹79 and ₹99 a month** in 2022 ([Senal News](https://senalnews.com/en/digital/crunchyroll-lowers-its-memberships-rates-in-india)). The entry tier is about US$0.94, among the cheapest prices it charges anywhere. Crunchyroll declines to disclose Indian subscribers. Sensor Tower counted **about 46,000 monthly active users of its Indian app** from January to mid-July 2024, against 15 million paid subscribers globally ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/crunchyroll-india-subscription-price/)). App users are not the whole audience, since the count leaves out web and TV viewing. Even so, 46,000 is about 0.03% of the 180 million fans the company claims. The same report notes that Crunchyroll lacked Indian rights to blockbusters such as Naruto.

Indians do pay for digital entertainment: paid video subscriptions nearly doubled to **216 million in 2025** ([EY/FICCI](https://www.ey.com/en_in/newsroom/2026/03/india-s-media-and-entertainment-sector-grew-9-percent-to-inr-2-point-78-trillion-in-2025-driven-by-digital-and-live-experiences-ficci-ey-report)). Their spending on anime specifically is small. Hakuhodo found the most common six-month anime spend was **₹500–1,000 for students and homemakers (36.8%) and ₹1,500–2,000 for working professionals (35.3%)**. Only 2–3% spent ₹6,000 or more, and fans preferred wearables to collectibles. Hakuhodo recommends localised content, trusted distribution and lifestyle merchandise ([MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/?p=2512580)).

Licensed merchandise is similarly small. The last public Licensing International figure put India at **US$1.396 billion of licensed retail sales in 2016, 20th in the world** ([The Week/IANS](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/brand-licensing-industry-sees-opportunity-for-growth.html)). That was about 0.5% of that year's US$262.9 billion global total. A Warner Bros. Discovery executive puts character and entertainment at about 26% of Indian licensing, against about 41% globally ([Indian Retailer](https://indianretailer.com/brandlicense/archives/interviews/india-evolving-licensing-market-vikram-sharma-warner-bros-discovery-india)). India's largest official pop-culture merch brand, The Souled Store, reported **₹492 crore of operating revenue in FY25 (+37%) but only ₹11 crore of profit**, across all its licensed and original lines ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/the-souled-store-nears-rs-500-cr-revenue-in-fy25-profits-decline-38-10954507)). That is about US$57 million at roughly ₹86 to the dollar, in a country where Crunchyroll claims 180 million anime fans. Globally, Licensing International credits anime and video games with driving 8% growth in the US$161.8 billion character and entertainment segment of a US$389.8 billion licensed market ([Total Licensing](https://www.totallicensing.com/licensing-reaches-389-8-billion-in-sales/)).

## Counterfeits and heavyweight licensing leave the small-deal middle empty

### Duties and a few gatekeepers make official goods scarce and expensive

Official Japanese merchandise reaches India through a narrow channel. Licensed figures typically cost **40–45% more than in Japan** because of import duties, so a US$40 figure sells for ₹5,000 or more. A Kolkata retailer blames counterfeits for a slump in its collectible sales ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/amp/story/pop-culture/pop-culture-originals/inside-indias-booming-anime-merchandise-trade)). Rights pass through a few intermediaries. **Crunchyroll** appointed Black White Orange as its Indian merchandise licensing agent for 35+ titles in 2023 ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/press-release/2023-10-13/crunchyroll-announces-merchandising-and-licensing-partnership-with-black-white-orange-in-india/.203381)), **Sony YAY!** named The Souled Store its direct partner for Naruto merchandise ([MediaInfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/entertainment/sony-yay-adds-naruto-to-its-licensing-merchandising-portfolio-in-india)), and The Souled Store's co-founder describes its Japanese anime licences as typically hard to acquire ([Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/article/retail-business/marvels/merch-maestro-vedang-patel-co-founder-souled-store-global-dreams)). **Bandai Namco India** relies on imported figures distributed by partners ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/bandai-namco-made-in-india-anime-merchandise)). An independent Indian creator with 50,000 followers has no visible route into this system.

### Fakes thrive in anime merch's core category and channel

No source measures the counterfeit share of anime merchandise in India, and judges should hear that plainly. The proxy evidence is strong, though. ASPA–CRISIL's 2025 survey of about 1,600 urban consumers in nine cities found that **35% had bought or encountered fakes in the past year and 31% had encountered counterfeit apparel**. It also found that **53% of counterfeit purchases happened online, and social-media ads accounted for 46% of fake apparel purchases** ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/indias-fake-goods-boom-9-in-10-urban-shoppers-have-bought-knockoffs-says-survey/2880659/)). The 2022 edition found that consumers believe 25–30% of the market is counterfeit. In that survey, 31% bought fakes knowingly, citing price and gaps between demand and supply, and 27% did not realise the product was fake ([PrintWeek India](https://www.printweek.in/news/25-30-of-the-market-in-india-is-counterfeit-aspa-crisil-report--57137)). Anime merch in India is mostly apparel, sold mostly online and promoted heavily on Instagram, which is the highest-risk category and channel in this data.

Applying the perceived 25–30% counterfeit share to Grand View's US$256 million anime-merch estimate gives an illustrative **US$64–110 million of leakage**. The range depends on whether the base already includes fakes. This is our arithmetic on perception data, not a measurement.

Globally, OECD and EUIPO put counterfeit trade at **US$467 billion in 2021, 2.3% of world imports** ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2025/05/global-trade-in-fake-goods-reached-USD-467-billion-posing-risks-to-consumer-safety-and-compromising-intellectual-property.html)). Clothing, footwear and leather made up 62% of seizures ([Global Finance](https://gfmag.com/economics-policy-regulation/fake-goods-trade-reaches-467-billion/)). Japan's METI measured merchandise for the first time and estimated **¥4.7 trillion (about US$30 billion) of 2025 damage from counterfeit Japanese anime and character goods**. That sits within ¥10.4 trillion of total piracy and counterfeit losses, and fake dolls and trading cards were the most serious problems ([TAPA APAC](https://tapa-apac.org/japan-estimates-usd-30-billion-losses-from-counterfeit-goods/); [Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/01/27/japan/crime-legal/digital-content-piracy-damages/)). The method counts fake consumption as lost sales, which critics dispute, and India was reportedly not among the countries surveyed. Verified authenticity can reach the deceived 27% directly. The 31% who buy fakes knowingly to save money are a price problem, and verification alone will not win them.

### Licensing terms are sized for licensees who sell tens of thousands of units

Anime licensing economics shut out small deals by design. For video rights, licensors want minimum guarantees large enough to make a deal worthwhile, and they favour established companies with a record of paying on time ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2021-08-02/how-much-does-it-cost-to-license-anime-series/.175579)). For merchandise, one licensing vendor describes minimum guarantees of **50–70% of projected first-year royalties**, apparel royalties of 8–12% and two- to three-year terms. It also describes **three approval stages that take two weeks to three months per item**, and 12–18 months to build a relationship with a licensor ([Vitrina](https://vitrina.ai/blog/anime-merchandising-licensing/)). That vendor is a single source with a commercial interest.

Approvals are the deeper bottleneck. Production committees must approve each new product unanimously and usually meet monthly. As a result, Hot Topic plans about 100 product types for a new show but often sees only a few arrive in time. Marubeni and Shogakukan's joint venture is testing AI to speed up reviews ([Briefs/Bloomberg](https://www.briefs.co/news/japan-s-big-trading-firms-target-24-billion-anime-goods-mark/)). Licensing International itself acknowledges that committee approvals cause delays ([Licensing International](https://licensinginternational.org/news/the-appeal-of-anime/)).

Now apply this to a creator drop. Five hundred tees at ₹1,000–1,500 with a 10% royalty earn the licensor **₹50,000–75,000, about US$580–870** (our arithmetic). That is less than the cost of drafting a contract and running three approval rounds. Itochu bought a 40% stake in Octas, a startup that licenses Japanese characters to North American manufacturers, because slow approvals left Western fans without authentic goods ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/itochu-boosts-anime-merchandise-foray-with-us-startup-octas)). Japanese capital sees the bottleneck, but it is fixing it at manufacturer scale, not creator scale.

### Fan-work rules already accept small runs, but exclude platforms

Japanese IP owners already accept small runs, as their fan-work guidelines show. **Nitroplus's** earlier guidelines let fans sell up to 200 units with revenue under ¥100,000 ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/send-us-your-doujinshi-nitroplus-affirms-positive-stance-on-fan-made-content-asks-people-to-show-them-what-theyre-cooking/)), and **Brown Dust 2** caps online sales at 100 units per work ([dotgg](https://dotgg.gg/brown-dust-2/%f0%9f%93%a2-derivative-works-guidelines-update-revised-on-july-4-2025/)). **hololive** requires no permission for fan activity that follows its rules, but bans corporate entities, including companies paying costs under an individual's name ([hololive](https://hololivepro.com/en/terms/)). **Coly** bans mass production and commercial use of official art and logos ([Coly](https://colyinc.com/en/guideline/)). The fan-creator economy these rules govern is large. pixiv reported 84 million members, about 4 million creators and 490,000 BOOTH stores in 2023 ([PayPal Newsroom](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2023-05-19-Pixivs-Online-Creative-Community-Continues-To-Grow-From-Japan-To-North-America)). The gap lies between two regimes. Fan-work rules allow small runs but forbid official assets and corporate involvement. Licences allow both, but only at scale. A platform that handles payment and production for a creator is exactly what the fan-work rules exclude. Torii therefore has to be licensor-sanctioned rather than operate in the grey zone, and it should borrow the controls these guidelines already use, such as unit caps and limits on what can be made.

## Every Torii mechanism already works somewhere

### Pay-first production is a Japanese norm, not an experiment

Producing only once enough orders arrive is mainstream in Japan's own anime-goods trade. **Makuake**, a listed pre-sale platform, processed **¥17.2 billion of "support purchases" in FY2025** on revenue of ¥4.58 billion (+25.3%), returned to operating profit, and passed ¥100 billion cumulative in November 2024. Its securities filing says reservation sales produce only the quantity needed and so reduce excess production ([edinetdb/EDINET](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E34557/text?fy=2025)). **Premium Bandai**, Bandai's preorder and made-to-order store, passed **5.55 million domestic members** in 2023 ([Zaikei](https://www.zaikei.co.jp/releases/2229806/)). BANDAI SPIRITS' Tamashii Web Shop closes orders when the prepared quantity is reached ([@Press](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/47419)), and **Good Smile** caps Nendoroid preorders at monthly factory capacity, reopening windows that ship later ([Good Smile](https://www.goodsmile.com/en/product/9758)). BANDAI SPIRITS is also reportedly moving popular reissues from lotteries to uncapped made-to-order sales to curb scalping, though this is a media report rather than an official announcement ([Toy People](https://www.toy-people.com/jp/?p=109379)). To a Japanese licensor, Torii's threshold preorder is the reservation (予約) model they already run, applied in a new market.

Outside Japan, all-or-nothing crowdfunding has raised **US$8.71 billion from 24.1 million backers** on Kickstarter ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickstarter)). About 42% of launched projects reach their goal ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/251727/projects-and-dollars-overview-on-crowdfunding-platform-kickstarter)). Comics have the highest success rate, at about 68% ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/251732/hulu-subscriber-number-us)). Both figures come from paywalled snippets. Kickstarter justifies all-or-nothing funding on two grounds: creators get enough money to deliver, and backers are charged only for viable projects. It also says it is not a store ([Kickstarter](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005047893-Why-is-funding-all-or-nothing-)). Torii's promise of a full refund on a defined product is a stronger guarantee to the buyer.

The cost being avoided is unsold stock. Estimates put **10–40% of garments made each year unsold** ([The Guardian](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-guardian-usa/20240119/282252375389251)). Producing only confirmed orders removes that risk for the creator. If licensors accept royalty-only terms, it also removes the minimum-guarantee risk for both sides.

Indian evidence of pay-first buying is thinner. Wishberry ran all-or-nothing rewards crowdfunding at small scale ([Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/company/wishberry)). Limited sneaker drops have sold out in minutes and crashed retailer sites ([NSS Magazine](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/24795/sneaker-game-india-adidas-nike)). No data exists on Indian anime-merch preorders.

### India's payment and production systems make a 200-unit run cheap to sell and make

Payments and production are not the constraint. UPI processed **24.07 billion transactions worth ₹29.37 lakh crore in September 2026** ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-processes-2407-billion-transactions-worth-rs-2937-lakh-crore-in-september)). It carried 85.5% of India's payment volume in the second half of 2025 ([Indian Television/RBI](https://indiantelevision.com/iworld/upi-drives-85-per-cent-of-indias-digital-payments-in-h2-2025/)). The **average merchant payment was ₹592 in 2025** ([IANS/Worldline](https://ianslive.in/upi-payments-surge-33-pc-to-2285-billion-transactions-in-2025-bharat-billpay-up-40-pc--20260406141107)), and about 86% of merchant payments are under ₹500 ([IIM Bangalore](https://www.iimb.ac.in/cdpg/pdf/digital-public-infrastructure-truly-scalable.pdf)). A ₹499–799 tee is an ordinary UPI purchase.

Production has no minimum order. Qikink lists a unisex tee base at **₹160 (₹177 with GST)** ([Qikink](https://qikink.com/)) and launched a free creator store with weekly payouts in 2026 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/07/qikink-launches-free-creator-store-power-indias-merchandise-economy)). Printrove opens an account against a refundable ₹5,000 recharge ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/printrove-print-on-demand-dropshipping)). Campaigns that outgrow print-on-demand can move to Tiruppur, whose knitwear cluster exported **₹44,747 crore (US$4.66 billion) in FY25** ([Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/manufacturing/tirupurs-export-growth-takes-off-can-maintain-pace/)). Because print-on-demand has no factory minimum, a Torii campaign's threshold can be set by what makes approval worthwhile for the licensor, not by a factory minimum order quantity.

Retail prices already match fan budgets. The Souled Store's licensed One Piece tee lists at ₹1,049 and sells for ₹545 on sale ([AJIO](https://www.ajio.com/c/onepiece-120826)). Bewakoof's anime oversized tees cost ₹549–649 ([Zoutons](https://zoutons.com/news/best-bewakoof-anime-t-shirts-naruto-one-piece-marvel-under-599-2026)). Given Hakuhodo's typical budgets, ₹499–799 is the price band that keeps students buying.

### Creators already drive commerce they are barely paid for

BCG counts **2–2.5 million monetised Indian creators who influence US$350–400 billion of consumer spending** and more than 30% of purchase decisions ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-from-content-to-commerce-mapping-indias-creator-economy)). Yet paid influencer marketing was only about **₹3,000–3,500 crore in 2025** ([The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2026/05/14/the-era-of-informal-influence-is-over-indias-creator-economy-is-entering-its-institutional-age.html)). Brand deals supply about 90% of creator income, a share BCG expects to fall to 70% by 2030 as commerce and other income streams grow ([NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/india-creator-economy-set-to-exceed-100-billion-usd-by-2030/)).

Anime fandom follows creators: 43.4% of Hakuhodo's respondents follow anime creators online ([MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/?p=2512580)). Indian creators who cover only anime are mid-sized. The Hindi explainer channel Animeranx, for example, has about 144,000 YouTube subscribers ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC4P7IgBNh_HSr75QYg95LcQ)). The realistic creator base is therefore many small and mid-sized Hindi-language creators rather than a few stars. Crunchyroll already works with Indian creators who have anywhere from about 100 to 35 million followers ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2023/07/gita-rebbapragada-on-crunchyroll-shaping-indias-anime-landscape)).

## A judge will probe five weak spots

**Thin fan budgets and unit economics.** The typical fan budget buys one or two tees every six months. Take a ₹649 tee: subtract the ₹177 print-on-demand base and a 10% royalty of about ₹65, and about ₹407 is left. Shipping, payment costs, taxes, the creator's share and the platform fee all still come out of that, and the evidence prices none of them. The category leaders are not very profitable. The Souled Store's net margin is about 2%, and Bewakoof lost ₹73.2 crore on ₹173 crore of FY25 revenue ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/bewakoof-narrows-fy25-loss-by-29-to-inr-73-2-cr)). Paying first does not remove returns, which ran at about a quarter of Bewakoof's gross sales ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/bewakoofs-growth-struggles-continue-in-fy25-but-losses-narrow-10610421)). Preorders also need buyers to pay up front, yet only about 41% of Indian e-commerce orders are prepaid ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/report/prepaid-e-commerce-orders-rise-10958917)). No data shows Indian fans trusting a weeks-long wait for goods they have paid for. Since most Kickstarter projects miss their goal, refunds will be routine, so they must be fast and cheap.

**The Indian data is weak where the pitch most wants it.** There is no audited figure for anime revenue in India. Analyst sizes disagree by 2x for anime and by about 22x for manga. The 180 million fans figure comes from Crunchyroll, and the Gen Z figure covers the whole sample, not India. No anime-specific counterfeit share exists, and METI apparently did not survey India. Even Infinity Castle's lifetime gross is unresolved, quoted anywhere from ₹66 crore net to ₹71.90 crore on a page labelled English-only ([Bollywood Hungama](https://www.bollywoodhungama.com/movie/demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-infinity-castle-english/box-office)). The pitch should therefore lead with Japanese primary data and evidence of what Indians actually do, rather than with Indian market sizes.

**Licensor appetite is unproven.** No source shows a Japanese licensor willing to approve creator designs in days or to accept royalty-only terms once demand is proven. No source reports the smallest deal Japanese licensors will accept from an Indian licensee. Production committees still need unanimity. Incumbents with exclusive or master rights, such as Black White Orange and The Souled Store, may see Torii as a competing channel. Rights are fragmented: Crunchyroll could not offer Naruto in India ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/crunchyroll-india-subscription-price/)). The most plausible first partners are IP owners with a single rights holder that already publish small-run fan-work rules, rather than titles owned by a production committee. That is our inference, not a sourced finding.

**Counterfeits will not disappear.** Verification wins the deceived buyer, not the 31% who choose fakes on price. Fakes pay no royalty, so they will always undercut official goods. Local production removes the 40–45% import-duty markup that drives some of that demand, but it does not close the price gap with goods that pay neither royalty nor duty.

**Big incumbents, and demand concentrated in a few hits.** Toei's ¥19 billion e-commerce and retail push, Bandai Namco's reported local apparel and The Souled Store's licence portfolio raise an obvious question: why would incumbents not do this themselves? The answer is that they serve the top franchises, while Torii serves the long tail of creators and mid-tier IP. But the box-office data shows demand clustering on a few franchises. *Chainsaw Man* took about a twelfth of *Demon Slayer*'s total, so campaigns for mid-tier IP will miss thresholds more often than the headline audience size suggests.

## Conclusion

The strongest version of this pitch reverses the usual order. Judges have heard "India is huge" many times, and the Indian market-size figures are the weakest numbers available. The Japanese numbers are the strongest: AJA's overseas split, the 3.3x export target, merchandising's 21% share at home, and METI's first measurement of counterfeit merchandise. So the argument should start from Japan's need. Japan has an export target it cannot reach through its existing markets, and its most mature way of earning from anime, merchandise licensing, barely operates in a market where 41% of people watch anime. Indian evidence then enters as observed behaviour rather than forecasts: box-office takings, sold-out events, UPI volumes, and creator influence. Framed this way, Torii is not a merch shop. It is a small-deal licensing layer that makes India's long tail worth a licensor's approval time.

A less obvious point is that Torii can also produce the data this research could not find. Each threshold campaign records verified, prepaid demand for a specific IP, design, price and city. No AJA, METI, JETRO or analyst source has that for India. Pitched to JETRO, that makes Torii useful measurement infrastructure for IMAGE-75 and the 2027 Year of Shared Horizons, as well as a revenue channel. The one unknown that decides whether this works is whether licensors will take part, and no amount of desk research can settle it. The credible ask is therefore a pilot. It would run with one or two IP owners whose fan-work rules already accept 100–200 unit runs, with a published asset kit and a handful of creator campaigns, timed to Mela! Mela! Anime Japan!! or IMAGE-75 events.
