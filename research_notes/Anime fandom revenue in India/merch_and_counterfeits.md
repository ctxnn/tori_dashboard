# Licensed / Character Merchandise in India and the Counterfeit Anime Merch "Leakage" Problem

Research date: 2026-10-24. Currency conversions, where given, are mine and labelled as such. Source quality flags: **[HARD]** = audited filing, government or intergovernmental estimate; **[SURVEY]** = consumer survey, self-reported; **[ANALYST]** = paid market-research estimate, often unverifiable; **[SELF]** = company or executive claim; **[WEAK]** = blog, retailer guide, press release or unattributed claim.

---

## 1. How big is licensed merchandise in India, and how fast is it growing?

### Takeaway
No current, public, authoritative India total exists. The last citable Licensing International (then LIMA) India figure is **US$1.396B in retail sales of licensed merchandise in 2016, ranking India 20th globally**. Later India numbers (~US$1.26B, ~Rs 4,000 crore) are undated or older, come from secondary sources and conflict with each other. Licensing International now reports only a "South Asia/PAC" region, which grew **+6.8% in 2024 and +5.3% in 2025**.

### Cited Findings
- **2016 baseline [HARD-ish, old]:** "India currently ranks 20 with $1,396 million retail sale of licensed merchandise in 2016." LIMA (now Licensing International) Global Licensing Industry Survey 2017, as quoted by LIMA's Maura Regan at the India Licensing Expo. Global market the same year: US$262.9B, +4.4%. — [The Week / IANS, Aug 29 2017](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/brand-licensing-industry-sees-opportunity-for-growth.html)
- At the time, LIMA projected India would reach "the level where China is today" in 5–7 years. A Viacom18 executive forecast at least 15% growth over five years. Both are 2017 forecasts and were never verified afterwards. — [The Week, 2017](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/brand-licensing-industry-sees-opportunity-for-growth.html)
- **Retail structure in 2017:** about 95% of Indian retail was traditional or unorganised. This structural reason helps explain why fakes are easy to sell. — [The Week, 2017](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/brand-licensing-industry-sees-opportunity-for-growth.html)
- **LIMA-based split [older, undated, secondary]:** Indian retail licensing of about US$1.26B, made up of entertainment US$406M, fashion US$594M and sports US$30M. Indian brands contribute only about 10% of licensing activity in India. Hollywood and US pop culture dominate. — [Apparel Resources, "Decoding the multi-million dollar character licensing industry in India"](https://in.apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/decoding-multi-million-dollar-character-licensing-industry-india/) (year not clear from the search snippet; the article appears to date from the mid-2010s)
- **Alternative domestic figure [WEAK]:** about Rs 4,000 crore domestic licensing, "80 percent dominated by characters and entertainment." — [Licensing Corner, "Changing tides of the Indian licensing industry"](https://licensingcorner.com/changing-tides-of-the-indian-licensing-industry/). This **conflicts** with the LIMA split above, where entertainment is about 32% (406/1,260), and with the WBD executive's 26% figure in Section 2.
- **Licensed sports merchandise only [ANALYST]:** IMARC puts the India licensed sports merchandise market at US$1,130.0M in 2025. That is far above LIMA's US$30M sports figure, so the two clearly use different definitions. IMARC probably includes team jerseys and fan apparel at retail. — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-licensed-sports-merchandise-market)
- **Regional growth (Licensing International):** South Asia/PAC +6.8% in 2024, which was above the market average. — [StartupTalky, citing Licensing International, Sep 2026](https://startuptalky.com/brand-licensing-in-india-how-businesses-can-turn-brand-equity-into-new-revenue/). South Asia/PAC +5.3% in 2025, below the global +5.45%. — [Total Licensing](https://www.totallicensing.com/licensing-reaches-389-8-billion-in-sales/)
- An LMCA India director (Sep 2026) describes India as "still an emerging market within this global licensing ecosystem." The same piece lists "Counterfeit products, unauthorised use of trademarks, unapproved products" as a key challenge for IP owners in India. — [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/brand-licensing-in-india-how-businesses-can-turn-brand-equity-into-new-revenue/)
- India-specific numbers are in the paid full Global Licensing Industry Study (free to members). — [Licensing International survey page](https://licensinginternational.org/get-survey/)

### Inferences
- In 2016 India was about **0.53%** of global licensed retail sales (1.396/262.9). If India had grown only at the South Asia/PAC pace (about 5–7%/yr), 2025 sales would be roughly US$2.3–2.6B. **This is my extrapolation, not a sourced figure.** It should be labelled as such if used.
- With global licensing at about US$390B and India at an estimated low-single-digit billions, India is heavily under-penetrated relative to its population. That supports the "headroom" argument but cannot replace a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No public India total from Licensing International after 2016. The 2024/2025 study country tables are paywalled.
- No reliable year-by-year India growth series.
- Not checked: India Licensing Expo / License India publications (Gaurav Marya), which may carry their own estimates.

## 2. Character/entertainment share, anime-specific merch in India, and seller revenue signals

### Takeaway
Globally, character/entertainment is about **41%** of licensed retail. One WBD executive puts India at about **26%**. India anime merch is sized at about **US$256M**, projected to **US$528M by 2030** (Grand View Research, quoted second-hand). The biggest visible official seller, **The Souled Store**, made **₹492 crore in operating revenue in FY25 (+37%)**, but it does not disclose its anime or licensed share. **Crunchyroll** has had a licensing agent in India (Black White Orange) since **Oct 2023** but has no store there. No India revenue data was found for Redwolf, Comic Sense, Bandai Namco, or Toei.

### Cited Findings
**Character/entertainment share**
- Global: entertainment/character is about 41% of licensing. India: "only 26%." This is an executive estimate, not audited data. — [Vikram Sharma, Warner Bros. Discovery India, SEA & Korea, interview in Indian Retailer (≈2024)](https://indianretailer.com/brandlicense/archives/interviews/india-evolving-licensing-market-vikram-sharma-warner-bros-discovery-india). The same interview cites piracy and distribution challenges as barriers to India's licensing growth (per search summary).
- The Licensing Corner "80% characters and entertainment" claim conflicts with this (see Section 1). — [Licensing Corner](https://licensingcorner.com/changing-tides-of-the-indian-licensing-industry/)

**Anime merch market size in India [ANALYST, second-hand]**
- India anime merchandise market: **about US$256M**, projected to reach **US$528M by 2030**, per Grand View Research as quoted by Outlook Respawn. The article itself warns that projections in fast-changing markets "should be viewed skeptically." The base year is not stated. — [Outlook Respawn, Aug 2025](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-originals/inside-indias-booming-anime-merchandise-trade)
- **Broader India anime market** (all revenue streams, not just merch; definitions vary widely):
  - IMARC: US$1,098M in 2024, rising to US$2,930M by 2033, an 11.5% CAGR. — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-anime-market)
  - Ken Research: US$2,107M in 2025, rising to US$4,447M by 2031. — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/india-anime-market.md)
  - The two differ by about 2x, so treat both as [ANALYST] and low confidence.

**Pricing / import-duty friction (a key driver of fakes)**
- Anime figures in India often cost **40–45% more than Japanese retail** because of import duties. A US$40 figure can retail for over ₹5,000 (about US$60). — [Outlook Respawn, Aug 2025](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-originals/inside-indias-booming-anime-merchandise-trade)
- One Kolkata retailer (F-Bloc) attributes a slump in its collectibles sales partly to counterfeits that make fans "less likely to purchase original items in India." Industry groups are lobbying for tax breaks and simpler customs on licensed collectibles. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-originals/inside-indias-booming-anime-merchandise-trade)
- Licensed anime SKUs reportedly sell at **about ₹1,000, versus ₹850 for original designs** (about an 18% premium). — [ANI press release via Business Standard, Jun 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/content/press-releases-ani/why-anime-merch-spending-is-exploding-as-india-goes-mainstream-126060800554_1.html) [WEAK: ANI press-release content, hedged with "reportedly"; I could not fetch the page directly, so this comes from the search snippet]
- The same release names The Souled Store, Bewakoof, Comicsense and Anime Devta as having "sizable anime catalogs" (Naruto, One Piece, Attack on Titan, DBZ). — [Business Standard / ANI](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/content/press-releases-ani/why-anime-merch-spending-is-exploding-as-india-goes-mainstream-126060800554_1.html) [WEAK]

**Bewakoof (owned by ABFRL's TMRW)**
- FY25 operating revenue ₹173 crore, +8% from ₹160.9 crore; net loss narrowed 29% to ₹73.2 crore from ₹103.1 crore. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/bewakoof-narrows-fy25-loss-by-29-to-inr-73-2-cr)

### Inferences
- TSS alone at ₹492 crore is about **US$57M** (my conversion at roughly ₹86/US$), and that covers all categories, not only anime. Grand View's US$256M for all India anime merch therefore looks plausible but narrow. It most likely excludes the unofficial and counterfeit market, though the definition is unknown.
- TSS's net margin is about **2.2%** (₹11 crore on ₹492 crore). Even the market leader runs thin on licensed merch. Royalties, procurement and duty costs compress margins, which leaves room for fakes that pay neither royalties nor duty to undercut on price.
- Licensing International says anime plus games, comics and social media make up 34% of global character/entertainment licensing (Section 5). India's lower 26% character share suggests anime is under-licensed in India relative to its fandom. This inference rests on one executive estimate.

### Gaps
- **No disclosed anime share of sales** for TSS, Bewakoof or anyone else.
- **No revenue data found for Redwolf or Comic Sense**, and no India-specific sales for **Bandai Namco, Toei, Shueisha or Tamashii Nations**.
- The original Grand View Research India anime merch report was not verified, and its base year is unknown.
- I could not fetch the ANI "anime merch spending exploding" release directly (403).

## 3. Counterfeit prevalence: India, Japan (METI/CODA) and global

### Takeaway
No hard, anime-specific counterfeit share exists for India. The best proxies are:
- **ASPA–CRISIL 2025:** 35% of urban Indians bought or encountered fakes in the past year; **31% reported counterfeit apparel**; **53% of counterfeit purchases happen online**; **social-media ads account for 46% of apparel fakes**; consumers perceive **about 25–30% of the market** as counterfeit.
- **Japan's METI, first-ever estimate:** **¥4.7 trillion (about US$30B) in 2025 damages from counterfeit Japanese anime and character merchandise worldwide**, inside a ¥10.4T total that includes digital piracy. Dolls/figures and trading cards are the worst categories. India was not named among the surveyed countries.
- **Global (OECD/EUIPO):** fakes were US$467B, or 2.3% of world imports in 2021; clothing, footwear and leather were 62% of seizures.

### Cited Findings
**India: ASPA–CRISIL "State of Counterfeiting in India 2025" [SURVEY]** (coverage dated Mar 2026)
- Sample: 1,600+ respondents (1,639 per another outlet) across 9 cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Indore), collected over 12 months. — [ThePrint, Mar 16 2026](https://theprint.in/feature/indias-fake-goods-boom-9-in-10-urban-shoppers-have-bought-knockoffs-says-survey/2880659/); [AgroSpectrum](https://agrospectrumindia.com/news/24/34679/89-of-indian-consumers-have-bought-counterfeit-product-aspacrisil-report.html)
- Prevalence: more than 35% bought or encountered fake products in the past 12 months, and about 89–90% have bought a fake at least once in their lifetime. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/indias-fake-goods-boom-9-in-10-urban-shoppers-have-bought-knockoffs-says-survey/2880659/)
- **Apparel: 31%** reported buying or encountering counterfeit apparel. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/indias-fake-goods-boom-9-in-10-urban-shoppers-have-bought-knockoffs-says-survey/2880659/)
- **Channels: online platforms account for 53% of counterfeit purchases. Social media ads are a major channel: 46% for apparel and 35% for consumer electronics.** — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/indias-fake-goods-boom-9-in-10-urban-shoppers-have-bought-knockoffs-says-survey/2880659/)
- Other categories: medicines 28% estimated fake, packaged food/personal/home care 27% encountered, auto components 22%, agro 35% of farmers. Also, 74% believe counterfeiting increased in the last 12 months. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/indias-fake-goods-boom-9-in-10-urban-shoppers-have-bought-knockoffs-says-survey/2880659/)
- Consumers estimate that **nearly 29%** of products in local markets could be counterfeit (a perception figure). — [ITVoice](https://www.itvoice.in/35-of-indians-encountered-fake-products-in-the-last-one-year-alone-aspa-crisil-state-of-counterfeiting-in-india-2025-report-reveals-alarming-trends); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/nearly-35-of-urban-consumers-in-india-bought-fake-products-in-the-last-year-report)
- Caveat: the sample is urban only, and "35% of Indians" is a sample-based extrapolation.

**India: ASPA–CRISIL "State of Counterfeiting in India 2022" [SURVEY]** (released Jan 2023; 12 cities; respondent count not given in coverage)
- Consumers perceive **25–30% of the market** as counterfeit. FMCG, apparel and agrochemicals are about 30%; pharma, automotive and consumer durables 20–25%. — [PrintWeek India, Jan 25 2023](https://www.printweek.in/news/25-30-of-the-market-in-india-is-counterfeit-aspa-crisil-report--57137)
- Where consumers encountered fakes: **apparel 31%, FMCG 28%, auto 25%, pharma 20%, durables 17%, agrochemicals 16%**. — [PrintWeek India](https://www.printweek.in/news/25-30-of-the-market-in-india-is-counterfeit-aspa-crisil-report--57137)
- About 27% did not know the product was fake at purchase; **31% willingly buy counterfeits**. The stated drivers were "price sensitivity, demand-supply gaps," desire for luxury brands and peer pressure. — [PrintWeek India](https://www.printweek.in/news/25-30-of-the-market-in-india-is-counterfeit-aspa-crisil-report--57137)

**India: FICCI CASCADE / TARI "Consuming the Illicit" [ANALYST/industry body]**
- Illicit market across five key industries: **US$95,329M per year**. **Textiles and apparel: US$48,268M in FY23**, up 29.67% from US$37,165M in 2017–18. Illicit trade grew faster in rural India. — [Apparel Resources, Sep 26 2024](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/illicit-market-key-indian-industries-valued-us-95329-million-says-ficci-report/)
- Caveats:
  - "Illicit" includes smuggling, tax evasion and grey market, not only counterfeits.
  - Verify the currency and definitions against the original FICCI report before quoting.
  - The FY23 apparel figure is the single most cited and least verified number here.
- Older FICCI figures (dated):
  - Smuggling losses across 7 sectors: Rs 1,05,381 crore in 2013–14, +44.4% versus 2011–12. — [FICCI press release](https://ficci.in/public/storage/PressRelease/2766/ficci-press-release-may15-smuggle.pdf)
  - Readymade garments output loss from smuggling: Rs 5,509 crore in 2017–18, up from Rs 3,780 crore in 2015–16. — [GKToday summary of FICCI "Enemy" study](https://www.gktoday.in/what-are-the-findings-of-the-study-enemy-impact-of/)

**Japan: METI / CODA estimates [HARD, government-commissioned]**
- **2025 (released Jan 26–27, 2026):** total estimated losses from piracy plus counterfeit goods tied to Japanese content were **¥10.4 trillion (about US$67B)**, inside and outside Japan. — [Japan Times, Jan 27 2026](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/01/27/japan/crime-legal/digital-content-piracy-damages/); [TAPA APAC](https://tapa-apac.org/japan-estimates-usd-30-billion-losses-from-counterfeit-goods/)
  - **Counterfeit goods: ¥4.7 trillion (about US$30B).** This is the first time the ministry measured merch. "Counterfeit dolls and trading cards" were the most serious problems. — [TAPA APAC](https://tapa-apac.org/japan-estimates-usd-30-billion-losses-from-counterfeit-goods/)
  - The survey was commissioned by METI and run by PwC Consulting for CODA. It followed METI's June 2025 action plan "to expand countermeasures not only for digital content but also for merchandise." — [Animenomics, Feb 4 2026](https://news.animenomics.com/p/counterfeit-anime-merchandise-cause-30-billion-dollars-in-damages)
  - Digital piracy was ¥5.7T, nearly 3x the 2022 level: print ¥2.6T, video ¥2.3T, games ¥0.5T, music ¥0.3T. — [TAPA APAC](https://tapa-apac.org/japan-estimates-usd-30-billion-losses-from-counterfeit-goods/)
  - Countries surveyed reportedly: Vietnam, France, Brazil, Japan, the US and China. **India is not among them.** — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/japan-loses-billions-2025-due-144700801.html) (from search summary only, not verified by full fetch. Unclear whether this list applies to the merch survey or the digital one.)
  - Critics note that "damage" methods equate pirated or fake consumption with lost sales, which is debatable. — [Sankaku Complex](https://news.sankakucomplex.com/n/_LyZzSbA0_lpNCYkcO5qrQ/) [WEAK source, but a valid methodological point]
- **Earlier CODA (digital only):**
  - 2021 losses ¥1.9–2.2T, about 5x the 2019 study. — [Anime News Network, Apr 2023](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interest/2023-04-26/coda-anime-manga-piracy-cost-industry-around-2-trillion-yen-in-2021/.197490)
  - 2022 losses about ¥1.95–2.20T (PwC for CODA). — [Asia Pacific Foundation of Canada](https://www.asiapacific.ca/publication/operation-anime-global-crackdown-pirated-japanese)

- Counterfeit goods were **US$467B in global trade in 2021, about 2.3% of world imports**. — [OECD press release, May 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2025/05/global-trade-in-fake-goods-reached-USD-467-billion-posing-risks-to-consumer-safety-and-compromising-intellectual-property.html)

### Inferences
- **Proxy for anime-merch leakage in India.** Anime merch is mostly apparel, sold mostly online and heavily via Instagram/social. That puts it in the **highest-risk category (apparel, 31%) and the highest-risk channel (online, 53%; social ads, 46% for apparel)** in the ASPA–CRISIL data. Applying the consumer-perceived 25–30% counterfeit share to the Grand View US$256M India anime merch figure gives two illustrative cases:
  - If US$256M is the total market including fakes, then about **US$64–77M** is counterfeit.
  - If it counts only legitimate sales, the fake market on top would be about **US$85–110M**.
  - **This is illustrative arithmetic on perception data, not a measured figure.** Anime fandom skews young and price-sensitive, so the true anime share could plausibly be higher than the economy-wide average. That is unproven.
- METI's merch estimate (¥4.7T) is about 45% of its total ¥10.4T. Japanese rights-holders now treat counterfeit merch as nearly as large a problem as digital piracy. This is a strong "why now" signal for verifiable official merch.
- Because India was apparently not among METI's surveyed countries, there is **no Japanese government number for India**. That is an open data gap a verifiable-merch platform could help fill.

### Gaps
- **No hard anime-specific counterfeit share for India** exists in any source found. The best proxies are the ASPA–CRISIL apparel and online figures above.
- No Amazon India, Flipkart or Meesho takedown or IP-complaint statistics specific to anime IP were found.
- Unverified: a retailer guide claim (surfaced in search) that fake figures cost ₹50–100 to produce and sell for ₹299–999 in India. The fetch timed out, so this is not citable.

## 4. Consumer evidence: willingness to pay for official merch, and buying fakes because official is unavailable or expensive

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly weak. One industry claim says Indian anime fans spend **40% more on licensed products than the national average** (Rakuten study, cited in a 2023 press release). One small academic survey says **75% prioritise authenticity**. On the other side, ASPA–CRISIL finds **31% willingly buy fakes**, driven by "price sensitivity, demand-supply gaps." That is the same tension as "official is too expensive or unavailable," but it is not anime-specific.

### Cited Findings
- "Anime enthusiasts in India ... are inclined to spend 40% more on licensed products than the national average." This is attributed to a Rakuten Digital Commerce study, quoted by Mitali Desai (COO, Black White Orange). No methodology is given. — [ANN press release, Oct 2023](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/press-release/2023-10-13/crunchyroll-announces-merchandising-and-licensing-partnership-with-black-white-orange-in-india/.203381) [SELF/WEAK]
- 75% of respondents "prioritize authenticity and official licensing" when buying anime merch online. About 40% prefer mid-range products (₹1,000–5,000) and 25% buy premium collectibles. The sample size and method are unknown (PDF fetch failed). — [JOIREM journal paper, "A Specialized E-Commerce Platform for Anime..."](https://joirem.com/wp-content/uploads/journal/published_paper/volume-10/issue-3/J_m8MBN8Td.pdf) [WEAK, likely a small student/academic sample]
- 31% of Indian consumers willingly buy counterfeit products. Drivers: price sensitivity, demand-supply gaps, aspiration and peer pressure. About 27% were unaware the product was fake at purchase. — [PrintWeek on ASPA–CRISIL 2022](https://www.printweek.in/news/25-30-of-the-market-in-india-is-counterfeit-aspa-crisil-report--57137) [SURVEY]
- 93% want more anti-counterfeit awareness campaigns, and 74% think counterfeiting is rising. — [ThePrint on ASPA–CRISIL 2025](https://theprint.in/feature/indias-fake-goods-boom-9-in-10-urban-shoppers-have-bought-knockoffs-says-survey/2880659/) [SURVEY]
- Qualitative evidence:
  - High prices for authentic items push budget-conscious fans toward fakes. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-originals/inside-indias-booming-anime-merchandise-trade)
  - Late-1990s Indian TV broadcasts (Pokémon, Beyblade, DBZ) created demand "that legitimate goods could not meet," which is the historical root of the unofficial market. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-originals/inside-indias-booming-anime-merchandise-trade)
  - Import duties add 40–45% to figure prices. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-originals/inside-indias-booming-anime-merchandise-trade)
- A widely repeated "79% of Indian anime viewers spend money on anime products" has **no traceable source**. Discard it. (Surfaced in search via a fan/retailer guide.)

### Inferences
- The evidence points to a demand-side split. A sizable segment values authenticity and pays a premium (TSS licensed SKUs at about ₹1,000 versus ₹850 for originals; the Rakuten "+40%" claim). Meanwhile, about a third of consumers knowingly buy fakes, and about a quarter are deceived. The deceived segment is the most directly addressable by **verifiability**. The price-driven segment is addressable by **availability and price-tiering**.

### Gaps
- **No large, independent survey of Indian anime fans** on official versus fake purchase rates, willingness to pay, or "couldn't find official" as a reason.
- I did not access the Rakuten study itself.
- I did not find any Crunchyroll, YouGov, Kantar or Redseer survey on Indian anime merch buying.

## 5. Global licensed merchandise market (Licensing International Global Licensing Industry Study)

### Takeaway
Global retail sales of licensed merchandise and services reached **US$389.8B in 2025 (+5.45%)**, ahead of global retail (+4.52%). **Character/entertainment is US$161.8B (+8%, about 41.5% share)**. Growth is explicitly "driven by ... strong contributions from the Anime and Video Game sectors." North Asia (Japan, Korea, China) is the fastest-growing region at **+14.1%**.

### Cited Findings
- **2025:** US$389.8B, +5.45% from US$369.6B in 2024. Global retail grew +4.52%. The study covered 51 countries and 1,068 companies. — [Total Licensing](https://www.totallicensing.com/licensing-reaches-389-8-billion-in-sales/); [License Global infographic 2026](https://www.licenseglobal.com/licensing-resources/the-global-licensing-industry-study-infographic-2026)
- **Character/Entertainment:** US$161.8B, +8%, "driven by healthy box office performance and strong contributions from the Anime and Video Game sectors." **Anime, Video Games, Comics, Social Media and Other together make up 34%** of character/entertainment licensing revenue. Film and TV make up 33%. — [Total Licensing](https://www.totallicensing.com/licensing-reaches-389-8-billion-in-sales/)
- Other categories: corporate brands about US$98B — [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/brand-licensing-in-india-how-businesses-can-turn-brand-equity-into-new-revenue/). Sports US$44.4B (+8.5%, an 11.4% share, its highest ever); licensed toys US$46.4B. — [Total Licensing](https://www.totallicensing.com/licensing-reaches-389-8-billion-in-sales/)
- **Regions:** North Asia +14.1% (fastest, led by Japan, South Korea, China); LATAM +7.6%; South Asia/PAC +5.3%. — [Total Licensing](https://www.totallicensing.com/licensing-reaches-389-8-billion-in-sales/)
- **Channels:** online retail is 32% of global licensed retail sales, and social media is 16% of online sales. — [Total Licensing](https://www.totallicensing.com/licensing-reaches-389-8-billion-in-sales/)
- **2024:** US$369.6B, +3.7% over 2023; 935 companies in 56 countries; conducted by Brandar Consulting. — [BusinessWire, May 20 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250520191636/en/Licensing-Internationals-2025-Global-Study-Shows-Licensing-Industry-Reached-$369.6-Billion)
- **2023:** US$356.5B, +4.6% over 2022. — [License Global](https://www.licenseglobal.com/licensing-resources/licensed-consumer-products-grow-to-356-5-billion-dollars)
- Japan's domestic anime merchandise market grew 6.8% year on year in 2024 (Association of Japanese Animations finding). — [Animenomics](https://news.animenomics.com/p/counterfeit-anime-merchandise-cause-30-billion-dollars-in-damages)

### Inferences
- The combined anime/games/comics/social bucket is about **US$55B** (34% × US$161.8B), but **anime alone is not broken out**.
- Benchmarked against legitimate licensed character retail (about US$162B globally), METI's ¥4.7T (about US$30B) counterfeit Japanese-merch estimate is very large relative to the anime slice. This frames counterfeits as a major leakage against official sales globally. The two figures use different methods and are only indicative.
- Social media is 16% of licensed online sales globally, while social-media ads drive 46% of counterfeit apparel purchases in India (ASPA–CRISIL). In India the social channel skews heavily toward fakes, which is the channel a verifiable-merch product must win.

### Gaps
- Anime-specific global licensed retail sales are not separated in public Licensing International materials.
- India's 2024/2025 country figure is behind the paywall (see Section 1).
- I could not fetch the License Global 2026 infographic directly (403); figures were taken from Total Licensing's write-up of the same study.
