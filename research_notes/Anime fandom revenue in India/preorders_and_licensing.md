# Pay-first fandom merch and licensing friction: evidence for threshold-funded production and fast small-collab approvals

Research date: 2026-10-24. Reliability flags are in brackets after each item: [PRIMARY] is the company or organisation's own page or filing; [SECONDARY] is press or an aggregator; [WEAK] is a vendor blog, a self-reported figure, an undated or paywalled snippet, or an item I could not open in full; [OLD] is data from before 2023, with the year given.

## Q1. Crowdfunding scale and success rates, and all-or-nothing funding as a demand test

### Takeaway
All-or-nothing crowdfunding is a proven mechanism at large scale. Kickstarter has collected about $8.7B in pledges from about 24M backers. Roughly 42% of launched projects fund. Comics fund most often, at about 60–68%. "Funds are collected only if the goal is met" is exactly the threshold-then-produce mechanic Torii proposes.

### Cited Findings
- Kickstarter totals: as of April 2025, US$8.71B pledged by 24.1M backers, funding 277,302 projects. [SECONDARY] (Wikipedia, citing Kickstarter's stats) — [Wikipedia: Kickstarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickstarter)
- As of January 2025, more than 651,000 projects had launched on Kickstarter and 41.98% were successfully funded. About $7.86B of all pledges went to successful projects. [WEAK] (Statista, paywalled, seen only as a snippet) — [Statista: Kickstarter projects and dollars overview](https://www.statista.com/statistics/251727/projects-and-dollars-overview-on-crowdfunding-platform-kickstarter)
- Success rate by category (January 2025): Comics had the highest rate at 67.65%, then Dance at 61.11% and Theater at 59.72%. [WEAK] (Statista snippet; the URL the search returned has a mislabeled slug) — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/251732/hulu-subscriber-number-us)
- An undated snapshot of Kickstarter's own stats page showed an overall success rate of 41.39%, Games at 49.41% and Technology at 23.28%. [WEAK] (undated). As of Oct 2026, kickstarter.com/help/stats redirects to the 2025 year-in-review post, so the live stats table could not be checked. — [Kickstarter stats (redirects)](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/stats)
- Older figures: Comics 57%, Theater 60%, Tech 20%, overall about 36.6%. [OLD, year not stated] — [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/kickstarter-crowdfunding-chances-of-success)
- Kickstarter called 2025 its "biggest year to date" but gave no total. Publishing raised more than $45M in 2025. *THE TRANSFORMERS* Compendium Set raised more than $4.6M and became the most-funded comics campaign in Kickstarter history. Open Calls had close to an 80% success rate and backed about 700 first-time and 800 returning creators. The post calls the EufyMake E1 printer the largest crowdfunding campaign in history, with no amount given. [PRIMARY, 23 Dec 2025] — [Kickstarter: A Year in Review 2025](https://updates.kickstarter.com/a-year-in-review-2025-kickstarter-highlights/)
- Kickstarter's stated reasons for all-or-nothing funding: creators get the money only if the goal is met, so they can be "confident that they have enough money to actually deliver what they promised". Backers are charged only if the campaign is viable. Kickstarter also says it is "not a store" and that "Backers are not making a guaranteed purchase." Fees are 5% of funds on successful projects plus about 3–5% for payment processing (Stripe). Campaigns run 1–60 days; 30 days "tends to work best". [PRIMARY] — [Kickstarter Help: Why is funding all-or-nothing?](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005047893-Why-is-funding-all-or-nothing-)
- Academic framing: Chemla & Tinn, "Learning Through Crowdfunding" (*Management Science*, 2020), model crowdfunding as a way for entrepreneurs to learn about demand before investing. A secondary summary says the mechanism is more efficient when information problems are large, and that the target should then be higher. [WEAK] (seen only through a reference list and a secondary summary; abstract not read) — [MPRA paper 111349 (cites Chemla & Tinn)](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/111349/1/MPRA_paper_111349.pdf)

### Inferences
- Comics and fan-oriented creative categories fund at about 1.5x the platform average. That suggests fan communities convert well when backing is all-or-nothing, which supports a fandom-merch focus for threshold campaigns.
- About 58% of Kickstarter projects do not fund. In Torii's model those projects never pay production costs, and that is the inventory-risk benefit. The refund flow will run often; it is not an edge case.
- Kickstarter's line that backers "are not making a guaranteed purchase" is a liability shield. Torii's promise of a full refund if the threshold is missed is a stronger consumer guarantee, and closer to Japanese 予約 (reservation) norms than to Kickstarter's "patronage" framing.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve Kickstarter's live category table (Design, Fashion, Games dollar totals and rates) for 2025–2026. The stats page now redirects.
- No primary study found that measures how well crowdfunding success predicts later retail sales (that is, demand validation quantified). Mollick (2014, *Journal of Business Venturing*) is often cited, but I did not open it.
- Statista figures are paywalled snippets and may be imprecise.

## Q2. Japan's preorder and made-to-order culture: Makuake, Premium Bandai, Good Smile, 受注生産 (made-to-order)

### Takeaway
In Japan, pay-first production is mainstream, not niche. Makuake runs about ¥17B a year in "support purchases" (応援購入) for products not yet in mass production, and passed ¥100B cumulative in Nov 2024. Its filings describe the reservation model as producing only what is needed. Premium Bandai had more than 5.55M domestic members by Oct 2023 and runs made-to-order (受注生産) windows for anime goods. Good Smile caps Nendoroid preorders by factory capacity and produces against orders.

### Cited Findings
**Makuake (TSE: 4479)**
- FY2025 (Oct 2024–Sep 2025): 応援購入総額 (total support purchases) was ¥17,221,652k (about ¥17.2B), up 3.8% YoY. 取扱高 (transaction volume, including the 安心システム purchase-protection fee) was ¥17,643,692k. Revenue was ¥4,578M (+25.3%). Operating profit was ¥447M, against an operating loss of ¥62.6M the year before. [SECONDARY] (EDINET filing text via edinetdb aggregator) — [edinetdb: Makuake securities report FY2025](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E34557/text?fy=2025)
- New project listings (掲載開始数, summed from quarterly figures): FY2022 8,314; FY2023 7,688; FY2024 6,370; FY2025 5,423. GMV per new listing, a derived proxy rather than a company KPI: about ¥2.38M (FY22), ¥2.29M (FY23), ¥2.61M (FY24), ¥3.18M (FY25). The company says it prioritised raising per-project value (1プロジェクト当たりの単価) over project count. [SECONDARY + my derivation] — [edinetdb](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E34557/text?fy=2025)

- Positioning in the filing: new products and services are sold on Makuake before mass production (量産前の新商品や新サービスをMakuake上で先行販売). Makuake describes itself as a "0次流通市場" (a "zero-th distribution" market that comes before normal retail). Projects are used for test marketing, customer-needs research and branding. The filing says the reservation-sales (予約販売) model produces only the quantity needed and so reduces excess production. [SECONDARY, search snippet; page returned 503 when fetched] — [gamebiz](https://gamebiz.jp/news/430136)

Makuake also gives project owners an analytics tool showing daily page views and supporter attributes. [SECONDARY] — [Commerce Pick](https://www.commercepick.com/?p=44962)

**Premium Bandai (プレミアムバンダイ) and BANDAI SPIRITS**
- Premium Bandai passed 5.55M domestic members, announced 19 Oct 2023. The service launched in Japan in April 2009. [SECONDARY, press release] — [Zaikei press release](https://www.zaikei.co.jp/releases/2229806/); [gamebiz](https://gamebiz.jp/news/372367)

- Bandai Namco Group FY2025 (year to Mar 2026) set records: net sales ¥1,348.2B, operating profit ¥189.5B. The Toy Hobby segment had ¥673.9B in sales. [PRIMARY] — [Good Smile product page](https://www.goodsmile.com/en/product/9758)
- The Gawr Gura (hololive) Nendoroid hit its preorder limit and closed early; a second window opened later. [SECONDARY] — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/second-round-of-gawr-gura-nendoroid-pre-orders-are-open/)
- The Minato Aqua Nendoroid was produced in limited quantity, with preorders closing once the cap was reached. [SECONDARY] — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/hololive-aqua-nendoroid-will-be-available-in-limited-quantities/)

### Inferences
- In Japan, fans accept paying weeks or months ahead and waiting for delivery as normal for anime goods. Premium Bandai alone has 5.55M+ members, and Makuake has processed ¥100B+. A Japanese IP owner is likely to see a threshold-preorder model as familiar, not risky. This matters for licensor buy-in.
- Makuake's own filing names reduced excess production as a benefit of reservation sales. That gives Torii a Japanese-language framing that licensors will recognise.
- BANDAI SPIRITS' reported shift from lottery to uncapped made-to-order suggests that even the largest licensors see pay-first, produce-to-demand as the better answer to scarcity and scalping.

### Gaps
- Premium Bandai's sales, made-to-order share, and member counts after 2023 are not disclosed in anything I found.
- Good Smile's preorder volumes and unit counts are not published.
- I could not fetch Makuake's official IR PDFs (JPX returned 403; one makuake.co.jp PDF returned 404). Figures come from the edinetdb aggregator of EDINET filings and should be checked against the primary 決算短信 (earnings summary).
- No quantified data on how widespread 受注生産 is across all anime merch (share of SKUs or revenue).

## Q3. India: crowdfunding, preorder and limited-drop evidence that Indian fans pay up front

### Takeaway
India has working rewards crowdfunding (Wishberry, all-or-nothing) and a large donation crowdfunding market (Ketto, ₹1,200+ crore). Limited drops sell out in minutes: sneaker releases sold out within minutes and crashed retailer sites. The fandom-apparel D2C category is growing fast: The Souled Store went from ₹360 crore in FY24 to ₹492 crore in FY25. Hard data on Indian anime-merch preorders specifically is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Wishberry, "the Kickstarter of India", runs all-or-nothing rewards campaigns. Creators get 60 days to hit their goal. Categories include film, music, theatre, comics, arts, publishing, games and design. Rewards include early-bird copies and limited-edition merchandise. [SECONDARY, about 2014–2018] — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/exclusive-wishberry-funding/)
- Wishberry scale: one profile reports ₹6.6 crore raised across 275+ projects from 12,000+ backers; an earlier one reports ₹3.5 crore for 150 projects from 8,500+ funders. It raised a Series A of about ₹10 crore ($1.4M) in 2018. [OLD 2016–2018; WEAK] (profile figures undated) — [Wellfound profile](https://wellfound.com/company/wishberry); [LetsVenture](https://letsventure.com/news/wishberry-raises-series-a-round); [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/?p=21015)
- Ketto has hosted more than 2 lakh (200,000) medical fundraisers, raised more than ₹1,200 crore, and built a community of more than 72 lakh (7.2M) donors. This is donation crowdfunding, not preorders, but it shows Indian users will pay online for a cause. [WEAK] (undated aggregator) — [CB Insights: Ketto](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/ketto-online-ventures)
- The Souled Store, an Indian fandom and licensed-merch D2C brand: FY24 revenue ₹360 crore (+54.5% from ₹233 crore in FY23), with 98.6% from product sales and net profit of ₹18 crore (after a ₹16.5 crore loss in FY23). [SECONDARY] — [Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/news/souled-store-reports-545-pc-revenue-growth-fy24-turns-profitable); FY25 revenue ₹492 crore — [Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/news/souled-store-reports-strong-fy25-growth-rs-492-cr-revenue)
- The Souled Store targets Gen Z with fandom collections that include anime characters such as Naruto. No anime-specific revenue figures. [SECONDARY] — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=449490)
- Sneaker drops in India: at one Yeezy release, the adidas India, VegNonVeg and Superkicks sites crashed under traffic, and the shoe (about ₹20,000–27,000 a pair) sold out within minutes. The article also notes that at the first Yeezy release in India (2016), no more than six people camped out. [OLD, about 2020] — [NSS Magazine](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/24795/sneaker-game-india-adidas-nike)

- Bengaluru sneaker brand Comet reportedly sells out 450-pair drops in 45 seconds. [WEAK] (self-reported by an investor on LinkedIn) — [LinkedIn](https://at.linkedin.com/in/aswinnarayan101)
- Crunchyroll named Black White Orange as its merchandising and licensing partner in India (Oct 2023). This means official anime merch rights in India are concentrated with a few master licensees. [SECONDARY, press release title; body not fetched] — [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/press-release/2023-10-13/crunchyroll-announces-merchandising-and-licensing-partnership-with-black-white-orange-in-india/.203381)

### Inferences
- Indian consumers show "pay now, scarce supply" behaviour in sneakers and growing spend on fandom apparel. A threshold-preorder anime drop sits between the two: drop scarcity plus a fandom premium.
- Wishberry's all-or-nothing model shows Indian creators and backers already understand threshold funding. Its small scale (single-digit crores in total) suggests that general-purpose crowdfunding has not scaled in India, which is a reason to go vertical on fandom.
- The Crunchyroll and Black White Orange master-licensee arrangement suggests that small Indian creators currently have no direct route to Japanese IP. That is the gap Torii's quick-approval layer would fill.

- No current (2023–2026) figures for Wishberry's activity; it may be dormant.
- I did not confirm whether Kickstarter accepts India-based creators, or how many Indian comics campaigns have funded.
- No quantified evidence on Indian consumers' trust in prepaying for goods that ship weeks later.

## Q4. Merch and apparel inventory risk: why pay-first reduces risk

### Takeaway
Credible estimates say 10–40% of garments made each year go unsold: roughly 8–60 billion items out of 80–150 billion. McKinsey and BoF estimate 2.5–5 billion excess items worth $70–140B in 2023 alone. Full-price sell-through of only about 50% is described as common. Producing only against confirmed orders removes this exposure.

### Cited Findings
- The Guardian (Jan 2024): 80–150 billion garments are made every year, and 10–40% of them are not sold. The paper stresses that the underlying data is weak. [SECONDARY, 2024] — [The Guardian via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-guardian-usa/20240119/282252375389251)
- A Source Fashion report (June 2025) says up to 40% of garments remain unsold, often ending in landfill, incineration or markdown bins. It also says only 1% of fashion brands are actively working to reduce production volumes. [SECONDARY, report by a trade show] — [The Industry Fashion](https://www.theindustry.fashion/new-report-urges-industry-overhaul-to-tackle-overproduction-crisis/); [Textination](https://textination.de/de/node/29324)
- McKinsey and BoF's *State of Fashion 2025* reportedly estimates 2.5–5 billion excess items produced in 2023, worth $70–140B. [WEAK] (seen only in secondary coverage; I could not open McKinsey's own page, which timed out) — [just-style](https://www.just-style.com/comment/the-digital-imperative-weaving-sustainability-collaboration-and-profit-into-fashions-future/); McKinsey press page (not loaded): [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/de/news/presse/2024-11-11-state-of-fashion-2025)
- *State of Fashion 2025* names cutting overstock and tightening buying decisions with data and AI as a top priority for the industry. [SECONDARY] — [CFDA summary](https://cfda.com/resources/sustainability-resource-hub/library-lexicon-annex/the-state-of-fashion-report-2025-mckinsey-company-and-bof-insights/)
- Full-price sell-through of only 50% "is now considered fairly common". [SECONDARY, opinion] — [The Interline](https://www.theinterline.com/?p=28183)
- A Charles University study of sustainability reports from 95 brands found that only about one-fifth even partially acknowledge unsold goods, so the true scale of the problem is likely under-reported. [SECONDARY, academic news] — [Charles University](https://iksz.fsv.cuni.cz/en/news/fashion-industry-strategically-stays-silent-unsold-goods-new-study-reveals)
- The Ellen MacArthur Foundation's *A New Textiles Economy* (28 Nov 2017) says clothing production roughly doubled over the previous 15 years and frames redesigning the system as a USD 500B economic opportunity. [PRIMARY, OLD 2017] — [Ellen MacArthur Foundation](https://www.ellenmacarthurfoundation.org/a-new-textiles-economy)
- Japanese counterpart: Makuake's filing describes its reservation model as making only the needed quantity, which reduces excess production. Good Smile caps production to orders and capacity. [SECONDARY / PRIMARY] — [edinetdb (Makuake filing)](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E34557/text?fy=2025); [Good Smile](https://www.goodsmile.com/en/product/9758)

### Inferences
- If a typical apparel or merch run has 10–40% unsold stock and only about 50% full-price sell-through, a pay-first model turns a 30–50% revenue-at-risk exposure into close to 0% for confirmed units. The remaining risk is the pre-campaign cost of design and samples.
- Licensed merch is worse than generic apparel because the licensee also owes the licensor's minimum guarantee whatever the sell-through (see Q5). Pay-first removes both inventory risk and guarantee risk if the licensor accepts royalty-only terms on validated campaigns.

### Gaps
- No data specific to anime or fandom merch on unsold rates or deadstock write-downs (for example, Indian merch D2C inventory days, or Japanese character-goods returns).
- The McKinsey and BoF figures are unverified against the primary report.
- I did not retrieve the European Environment Agency's estimate of textiles destroyed before use.

## Q5. Licensing friction: timelines, approval cycles, minimum guarantees, and Japanese licensors

### Takeaway
Licensing is a ~$390B market built around large licensees. Standard terms are minimum guarantees (MGs) sized as 50–70% of projected first-year royalties (a vendor example: $250–350K), deals of 2–3 years, 12–18 months to build a licensor relationship, and multi-stage approvals of about 2 weeks to 3 months per product. Japanese anime IP adds production-committee consensus, and Licensing International says creator and committee approvals cause delays. The structure excludes a creator who wants to sell 200 hoodies.

### Cited Findings
- Licensing International's Global Licensing Survey 2025 puts global licensed merchandise and services sales at about $389.8B, up 5.45% from $369.6B. Entertainment and characters are the largest segment at $161.8B (41.5%). The full report costs $5,500 for non-members. Note: the $369.6B was the previous survey's figure, so $389.8B most likely refers to calendar-2024 sales. [PRIMARY, summary page] — [Licensing International: Global Licensing Survey](https://licensinginternational.org/global-licensing-survey/)
- Licensing International ("The Appeal of Anime"): licensed anime merchandise needs approval from content creators and production committees in Japan, and these approvals can cause delays. Content may be in development for 3–4 years, which makes merchandise programmes hard to mount. Merchandise may not reach market until about six months after an anime airs. [WEAK] (the page blocked automated fetch; content comes from a search snippet; article date unknown) — [Licensing International](https://licensinginternational.org/news/the-appeal-of-anime/)
- Production committees (製作委員会): each member has a stake, so all must agree on a licensing offer, and a single holdout can stall the negotiation. [SECONDARY / OLD] — [AnimeNation "Ask John"](https://www.animenation.net/blog/?p=28945); [Vitrina explainer](https://vitrina.ai/?p=141408)
- Justin Sevakis, a former anime licensing and distribution executive (Anime News Network, 2015): licensors must approve every pack-in item, toy and T-shirt, whether sold or given away as promotion. Some properties get far more scrutiny than others, and he calls approvals the most frustrating part of working in the anime industry. [SECONDARY, OLD 2015] — [Anime News Network Answerman](https://Animenewsnetwork.Com/answerman/2015-07-31/.91097)
- Anime-merch licensing terms (Vitrina, Aug 2026; vendor blog [WEAK]):
  - MGs are set at 50–70% of projected first-year royalties; for example, $500K of projected royalties implies an MG of $250–350K.
  - Royalties run 10–15% for collectible figures and 8–12% for apparel and soft goods.
  - Approvals take about 2 weeks for simple items and up to 3 months for complex collectibles; the post recommends a buffer of at least 90 days. There are three stages: concept, pre-production sample, final production.
  - Deals typically run 2–3 years, and building a licensor relationship independently takes 12–18 months.
  - Agent fees are 10–15% of royalties.
  - The post names Toei, Bandai Namco and Aniplex as each having its own approval and sub-licensing structure.
  - Source: [Vitrina: anime merchandising licensing](https://vitrina.ai/blog/anime-merchandising-licensing/)
- In general licensing, the advance paid at signing is often 25–50% of the MG. [WEAK] (inventor-licensing context) — [inventRight](https://inventright.com/innovate-this/how-to-negotiate-minimum-guarantees)
- Standard approval stages in merchandise licences: concept drawings or layouts, artwork samples, pre-production samples, final production samples, and advertising materials. Counsel advise agreeing the process up front so that late approvals don't put the licensee in breach. [SECONDARY, OLD 2022] — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/fr/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/02/merchandise-license-agreements)
- A slow approval process and limited overseas distribution leave many Western fans unable to find authentic anime merch. In response, Itochu took a 40% stake in Octas, a San Francisco character-licensing startup that licenses Japanese characters to North American manufacturers. [SECONDARY] — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/itochu-boosts-anime-merchandise-foray-with-us-startup-octas); [Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/?p=10455)

### Inferences
- Rough arithmetic: an MG of even $25K, a tenth of the vendor example, is more than the gross royalty on about 2,000–3,000 apparel units at ₹1,000–1,500 with a 10% royalty. Under current terms a 200–500 unit creator collab cannot be licensed economically. That is the clearest "too heavy for small deals" argument.
- If each SKU needs 2 weeks to 3 months of approval across three stages, a creator drop that should take 2–4 weeks ends up taking a quarter. A licensor-side workflow with pre-approved templates, one-round approval, a hash-bound approved version (which Torii's immutable `content_hash` approvals already model) and royalty-only terms on validated demand would remove the two biggest frictions, MG and approval latency.
- Itochu's Octas investment shows that big Japanese players see the overseas approval and distribution bottleneck as worth fixing, but at the level of manufacturing licensees, not creator-scale deals.

### Gaps
- No primary industry survey found that quantifies approval turnaround (median days, number of rounds) or the share of licence applications rejected for being too small. The Vitrina figures are a single vendor's estimates.
- No Japanese-language source found giving typical 監修 (approval review) durations or MG norms for Japanese licensors (版元, the rights holders).
- No source found on minimum deal sizes Japanese licensors will accept from Indian licensees.

## Q6. The fan-work (二次創作) gray zone and official programmes that let fans and creators sell

### Takeaway
Japanese IP owners increasingly publish fan-work (二次創作) guidelines that allow individuals to sell small runs with no application: hololive/COVER, Nitroplus (previously ≤200 units and <¥100K), Brown Dust 2 (≤100 units online) and Arc System Works. These guidelines typically ban corporate involvement, mass production and official-asset use. The resulting fan-commerce economy is large: BOOTH has 490K+ store registrations, pixiv has 84M+ members and about 4M creators, and Comiket draws about 250K people per event. Licensors evidently tolerate small-scale fan commerce, but there is no sanctioned route between fan-work rules and full licensing. A fast small-collab approval product would fill that space.

### Cited Findings
**Official guidelines**
- hololive / COVER (derivative-works guidelines, rev. 2026-08-31):
  - Derivative works are limited to "fan or hobby activities", and business-purpose activity is prohibited.
  - "There is no need to contact us for permission" when the guidelines are followed.
  - Activity by a corporate entity is prohibited, including when a company bears the costs under an individual's name.
  - Paid fan games require an application to the holo Indie programme.
  - Figures and garage kits need separate rights clearance.
  - There are no unit or yen caps, but COVER may ask for income disclosure, or cancellation, at fan events with high profit.
  - [PRIMARY] — [hololive production terms](https://hololivepro.com/en/terms/)
- In Nov 2023, hololive updated its policy so indie developers can sell fan-made games, which had previously been free-only. [SECONDARY, 2023] — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20231116_23059/)
- Nitroplus's earlier guidelines allowed direct sales of fan works with cumulative sales of 200 units or fewer and expected revenue under ¥100,000. It later moved to a looser standard that bars only "excessively commercial" projects, and now invites fans to send in doujinshi (self-published fan works). [SECONDARY] — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/send-us-your-doujinshi-nitroplus-affirms-positive-stance-on-fan-made-content-asks-people-to-show-them-what-theyre-cooking/)
- Brown Dust 2 (rev. July 2025): online sales capped at 100 units per derivative work, sold directly by the creator rather than through third-party distributors. [SECONDARY, 2025] — [dotgg](https://dotgg.gg/brown-dust-2/%f0%9f%93%a2-derivative-works-guidelines-update-revised-on-july-4-2025/)
- Coly Inc.: prohibits selling goods intended for mass production or mass sale, and commercial use of official illustrations or logos. [PRIMARY] — [Coly guideline](https://colyinc.com/en/guideline/)
- Arc System Works: fan-content guidelines cover individuals and unincorporated groups, including doujin circles, and compliant activity needs "no application process or notification". [PRIMARY] — [Arc System Works](https://www.arcsystemworks.jp/?p=31367)
- Nintendo (2018) replaced its Creators Program with sign-up-free guidelines for video and image content. This does not cover merch or doujin sales. [SECONDARY, OLD 2018] — [Nintendojo](https://www.nintendojo.com/?p=173189)

**Scale of fan-creator commerce**
- BOOTH (pixiv's creator marketplace) had 490,000 store postings and registrations as of May 2023, per pixiv CEO Shingo Kunieda. The same release gives pixiv 84M members, about 4M creators and 120M+ works. Japan accounted for 35.6% of new registrations, and North America and China about 15% each. pixivFANBOX has about 10M registrants and about 190K creators. [PRIMARY, press release, May 2023] — [PayPal Newsroom](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2023-05-19-Pixivs-Online-Creative-Community-Continues-To-Grow-From-Japan-To-North-America)
- pixiv's total registered users were reported to pass 119M (at pixiv's 18th anniversary). [SECONDARY] — [Koubo](https://koubo.jp/article/45924)
- BOOTH's 3D-model category alone did about ¥2.4B in GMV and about 1.48M orders in 2022. [SECONDARY, OLD 2022] — [MoguraVR](https://www.moguravr.com/?p=212613)
- Comic Market 106 (16–17 Aug 2025, Tokyo Big Sight) drew about 250,000 attendees over two days. [SECONDARY] (Anime News Network headline, seen through search; exact article URL not confirmed) — [Anime News Network: Comiket](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/encyclopedia/company.php?id=9272). Comiket C101 (end of 2022) had about 20,000 exhibiting circles. [SECONDARY, OLD 2022] — [KAI-YOU](https://kai-you.net/article/85685)

### Inferences
- Japanese IP owners already tolerate, and increasingly encourage, small-run fan commerce: caps of 100–200 units, no application needed. That shows they accept small deals in principle. Their objections are to corporate involvement, mass production and loss of control.
- Torii's "small creator collab" sits in a structural hole. It is too commercial and too corporate for fan-work guidelines (a platform company handling payments and production is exactly what hololive's "corporate entity" clause excludes), and too small for MG-based licensing. A licensor-controlled, per-campaign approval with caps (for example, threshold plus a maximum unit count), royalty-only economics and a binding approved-design hash maps directly onto the controls these guidelines already use.
- pixiv and BOOTH's international registration mix (about 65% of new members outside Japan) suggests overseas fans, including India, are part of the fan-creator economy. Their purchasing volume is not quantified.

### Gaps
