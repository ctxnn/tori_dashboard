# Japan side: global anime market, overseas revenue, government export targets, and Japan–India content cooperation

Research date: 24 Oct 2026. USD conversions are approximate. Unless a source gives its own conversion, I use about ¥150 = US$1 for 2023–2025 figures. Figures marked (weak) come from aggregators, marketing vendors, unnamed sources or search snippets I could not open in full.

## 1. AJA Anime Industry Report: total market, overseas share, overseas revenue, growth (2023 and 2024 data)

### Takeaway
Overseas revenue is now the larger half of Japan's anime market, and it is where the growth comes from. In 2024 the wide-definition market hit a record ¥3.84 trillion (about US$25B). Overseas was ¥2.17 trillion (56.5%, +26% YoY), while domestic grew only 2.8%. In 2023 overseas first passed domestic in a "normal" year, at ¥1.72 trillion (51.5% share).

### Cited Findings
**2024 data (AJA "Anime Industry Report 2025"; summary presented at TIFFCOM, Oct 2025; full report Dec 2025)**
- Total anime industry market (wide definition): ¥3.8407 trillion (about US$25.1B), a record. Growth was +14.8%, the second-highest annual increase after 2019 (+15.3%). — [Anime News Network, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)
- Domestic: ¥1.6705 trillion (about US$10.9B), +2.8%. Overseas: ¥2.1702 trillion (about US$14.1B), +26%. — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)
- Overseas share was 56.5% of the total. 2024 was the second year in a row that overseas exceeded domestic. — [crex-data (aggregator citing AJA)](https://www.crex-data.com/industry/media-it/anime/market-size). The share also follows directly from ANN's figures (2.1702 / 3.8407).
- 2024 was the third year overseas exceeded domestic, after 2020 and 2023. — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)
- AJA's lead author said the overseas market "now far exceeds local revenues, and this gap will only widen." — [ANN via search summary](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)
- Production-side ("industry market", narrow definition, revenue of the anime production companies themselves):
  - Total ¥466.2B (about US$3.0B), +9.1%, a record.
  - Domestic ¥347.4B (+6.7%).
  - Overseas ¥118.8B (about US$770M, +16.6%), about 25.5% of studio revenue.
  — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)
- Note on reporting style: Japanese sources write growth as a ratio to the prior year. "前年比114.8%" means +14.8% and "126.0%" means +26%. Some English outlets (Automaton, Popverse) printed "114.8%" and "126%" growth, which is a misreading, not a conflicting figure. — [crex-data](https://www.crex-data.com/industry/media-it/anime/market-size); [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/overseas-anime-market-growth-continues-to-outpace-domestic-market-gap-in-revenue-expected-to-grow-industry-research-shows/)
- Also covered by [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/global/markets-festivals/japans-anime-market-25-billion-global-boom-1236565413) (headline "$25 billion").

**2023 data (AJA "Anime Industry Report 2024"; launch seminar 20 Dec 2024)**
- Total ¥3.3465 trillion (about US$21–22B), +14.3% YoY, a record at the time. Production-side industry market was ¥427.2B (+25.4%). — [anime.eiga.com, Feb 2025](https://anime.eiga.com/news/123132/)
- Overseas ¥1.7222 trillion (+18.0%). Domestic ¥1.6243 trillion. Overseas share 51.5%. — [anime.eiga.com](https://anime.eiga.com/news/123132/); [ANN](https://animenewsnetwork.com/cms/.219656); [SoraNews24, Jan 2025](https://soranews24.com/2025/01/09/anime-industry-report-shows-overseas-anime-market-is-bigger-than-japanese-one-but-is-this-a-cultural-tipping-point/amp/)

**Cross-check from a second source (Human Media, Nov 2025)**
- Human Media separately estimates 2024 overseas anime sales at about ¥2.2 trillion (+26%). This includes theatrical, broadcast and streaming video plus merchandising licensing. — [GameBusiness.jp, 1 Dec 2025](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/12/01/25630.html)

**2025 data**
- I found no published AJA figure for 2025. One outlet extrapolated "over ¥4 trillion" if the 2024 pace continued. That is an estimate, not data. — [search summary of AJA coverage](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/overseas-anime-market-growth-continues-to-outpace-domestic-market-gap-in-revenue-expected-to-grow-industry-research-shows/) (weak)

### Inferences
- From 2023 to 2024 the total market grew by about ¥494B, and overseas grew by about ¥448B. Overseas therefore accounted for about 91% of all 2024 market growth (my calculation from AJA figures).
- Overseas went from 51.5% to 56.5% share in one year. If the gap between 26% overseas growth and 2.8% domestic growth continues, overseas would reach about 60% of the market by 2025–26. This is an extrapolation, not a forecast from AJA.
- Studios themselves earn only about 25% of their revenue overseas, against 56% for the wider market. Much of the overseas value goes to licensors, platforms and committee members rather than studios. Overseas licensing income still flows mainly through Japanese rights holders.

### Gaps
- AJA's English summary PDF for the 2025 report was not retrieved. Figures come from ANN, eiga.com and crex-data coverage.
- The AJA Anime Industry Report 2026 (2025 data) may be presented at TIFFCOM around late Oct/Nov 2026, but no results surfaced as of the research date.
- I found no regional split of AJA's overseas figure (North America vs China vs Asia vs India). The public summaries do not break out India.

## 2. Share of the anime market from merchandising/licensing

### Takeaway
Domestic merchandising (商品化) is the largest single domestic segment: ¥700.8B in 2023, about 21% of the whole wide-definition market and about 43% of domestic. The overseas segment also bundles merchandise licensing, so licensing-based revenue is a large share of the total.

### Cited Findings
- AJA 2023 segment breakdown (wide definition, ¥3.3465T total; growth vs 2022 in brackets) — [anime.eiga.com](https://anime.eiga.com/news/123132/):
  - Overseas: ¥1,722.2B (+18.0%)
  - Merchandising (商品化): ¥700.8B (+4.7%)
  - Amusement (遊興, mainly pachinko/pachislot): ¥337.0B (+13.1%)
  - Streaming (配信): ¥250.1B (+51.4%)
  - Live entertainment: ¥108.1B (+11.2%)
  - TV: ¥97.3B (+3.2%)
  - Film: ¥68.1B (−13.3%)
  - Video packages: ¥36.2B (−6.0%)
  - Music: ¥26.7B (−2.6%)
- Statista describes merchandising as "by far the largest domestic segment." — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1093754/japan-animation-industry-revenue-by-segment/%20)
- Human Media's overseas anime figure (about ¥2.2T, 2024) explicitly includes merchandising licensing alongside video. — [GameBusiness.jp](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/12/01/25630.html)
- Global licensed anime merchandise is estimated at about US$13B today. Japanese trading houses (Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Sumitomo, Itochu) see a US$24B opportunity by 2033, citing Grand View Research. — [Briefs Finance, 1 Jul 2026, citing Bloomberg](https://www.briefs.co/news/japan-s-big-trading-firms-target-24-billion-anime-goods-mark/) (market-research estimate, weak)
- Global context: Licensing International reports retail sales of licensed merchandise and services of US$389.8B in 2025 (+5.45%). Character/Entertainment is the leading property type and apparel the largest category. — [License Global](https://www.licenseglobal.com/licensing-resources/the-global-licensing-industry-study-infographic-2026)

### Inferences
- In 2023, merchandising was 700.8 / 3346.5, about 20.9% of the total market and 43.1% of the domestic market (my calculation).
- In Japan, merchandise is worth nearly 3x streaming, and streaming is growing about 10x faster. Merchandise licensing is the most mature monetisation lever. In markets like India, where streaming ARPU is very low (see Q3), merchandise licensing is the closest analogue to how anime earns money at home.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the AJA 2024-data (Report 2025) segment breakdown, i.e. merchandising for 2024.
- I found no split of AJA's overseas ¥2.17T into streaming, merchandise and other.

## 3. Japan government content-export targets (Cool Japan, 20 trillion yen by 2033, current overseas sales)

### Takeaway
Japan's government has made content exports an explicit growth pillar. The revised Cool Japan Strategy (June 2024) targets ¥20 trillion (about US$130B) in overseas sales of Japanese content by 2033. Overseas content sales were ¥4.7T in 2022, about ¥5.8T in 2023 and about ¥6.0T in 2024. On that path the target needs roughly 3.3–3.7x growth in under a decade. Anime is about 36% of current overseas content sales.

### Cited Findings
- **Revised Cool Japan Strategy (June 2024)**
  - Targets annual overseas sales of Japanese content (games, anime, manga) of ¥20 trillion (nearly US$130B) by 2033, up from ¥4.7T (about US$30B) in 2022. That is roughly a 4x increase.
  - A broader goal of ¥50 trillion by 2033 adds fashion, cosmetics and inbound tourism.
  - Comparison: 2022 microchip exports were ¥5.7T.
  - The strategy calls stronger anti-piracy measures "essential to expanding the global market."
  — [AFP via AFPBB, 5 Jun 2024](https://www.afpbb.com/articles/-/3522932)
- 2023 overseas sales of Japanese content were about ¥5.8T, surpassing Japan's semiconductor exports. — [Jiji via Adnkronos, 13 Jan 2026](https://english.adnkronos.com/2026/01/13/tokyo-report-japan-to-boost-aid-for-games-anime-as-new-pillar-of-growth/)
- 2024 overseas entertainment sales were ¥6.044T (about US$39B). The government's interim milestone is an estimated ¥10T in 2028, then ¥20T in 2033. The 2033 target is "3.7 times the sales in 2024." — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)
- Human Media's 2024 estimate for the same seven fields is similar:
  - Overseas sales about ¥6T (+4%), of which about ¥3.4T is the estimated return flow to Japan.
  - Anime is about ¥2.2T.
  - The domestic content market is ¥15.26T (+3.9%).
  — [GameBusiness.jp, 1 Dec 2025](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/12/01/25630.html)
- Another report gives FY2024 overseas sales of ¥6.1T. — [search summary of Automaton/Jiji coverage](https://automaton-media.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/68709) (weak; slightly inconsistent with ¥6.044T, likely a fiscal vs calendar year difference)
- **Budgets**
  - Government support for the content industry is ¥25.3B. Comparisons: South Korea about ¥76B (2023), US about ¥600B.
  - An LDP emergency resolution calls for "more than ¥100 billion."
  - The Takaichi administration put ¥35B in the FY2025 supplementary budget for large-scale multi-year support.
  - A third-party certification body for working conditions is targeted for FY2028.
  — [Jiji via Adnkronos, 13 Jan 2026](https://english.adnkronos.com/2026/01/13/tokyo-report-japan-to-boost-aid-for-games-anime-as-new-pillar-of-growth/)
- Sept 2025: a ministerial meeting chaired by Chief Cabinet Secretary Hayashi compiled a package of multi-year support for overseas expansion of digital content (anime, film). It reaffirmed the public-private target of ¥20T by 2033. — [Jiji via Adnkronos, 20 Sep 2025](https://english.adnkronos.com/2025/09/20/japan-govt-to-support-domestic-digital-content-overseas/)
- The government plans a centralized organization to support global expansion of games, anime and manga. — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/japanese-government-to-launch-a-centralized-organization-to-support-the-global-expansion-of-games-anime-and-manga/) (headline only; details not retrieved)
- METI's entertainment and creative industry council materials put Japan's domestic content market at ¥13.1T in 2022, third in the world. — [METI council PDF](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/mono_info_service/entertainment_creative/pdf/001_04_00.pdf) (from search snippet)
- Industry follows the policy: Bandai Namco Filmworks aims to lift the overseas share of its screen-release revenue from 25% (FY2023) to 50% (FY2030). — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-10-30/global-anime-market-grew-15-percent-to-record-3.84-trillion-yen-in-2024/.230464)

### Inferences
- Reaching ¥20T from about ¥6T in 2024 needs about 14% CAGR over 2024–2033. AJA's overseas anime segment grew 18% (2023) and 26% (2024), but total content exports grew only about 4% in 2024 (Human Media). On that basis, the target requires new markets and new revenue lines, not just growth in the US and China. For a pitch, India is a natural answer.
- Anime is about ¥2.2T of about ¥6.0T, roughly 36% of overseas content sales. Games make up most of the rest (inferred; no breakdown found).

### Gaps
- I did not open the Cabinet Office primary Cool Japan Strategy 2024 document ([cao.go.jp PDF found](https://www.cao.go.jp/cool_japan/report/000127553.pdf)). Figures above are from wire coverage.
- I found no official sector breakdown (games, anime, publishing, film) of the ¥5.8T or ¥6.0T overseas totals.
- I found no official name or start date for the planned "centralized organization."

## 4. India / South Asia share of Japanese content revenue; statements that India is a priority or underserved

### Takeaway
No Japanese source publishes India's share of anime revenue, which itself shows how small it is. The evidence points to a very large audience earning very little. India is called the #2 (Crunchyroll, 2023) or #3 (Hakuhodo, 2026) anime viewing market. Yet Crunchyroll prices at ₹79/month (about US$0.94), will not disclose subscribers, and its app had only about 46,000 monthly active users in 2024. Japanese majors (Toei, Bandai Namco, Sony/Crunchyroll) now publicly name India a priority.

### Cited Findings
**Audience size vs monetisation**
- Crunchyroll SVP Mitchel Berger called India "the number 2 market for us", on viewership not revenue. He said India would "represent 60 per cent of the anime viewership growth" in coming years. Crunchyroll had 500 shows and 3,800 hours for India; prices were ₹79 and ₹99 per month; Hindi-dub engagement was 2.5x English. — [t2ONLINE, 28 Jul 2023](https://t2online.in/screen/streaming/global-anime-brand-crunchyroll-sees-india-as-the-second-largest-anime-market-in-the-world-after-the-us/508339)
- Crunchyroll's Indian price starts at ₹79/month (about US$0.94), "among the cheapest in the world."
  - Crunchyroll declined to share Indian subscriber numbers.
  - Sensor Tower: its mobile app had 46,000 MAU in India (1 Jan–17 Jul 2024), up from 29,000 in 2021. Global paid subscribers were 15M.
  - Dubbed titles are over 65% of Indian viewership.
  - Monetisation barriers: subscription fatigue; blockbuster titles such as Naruto unavailable because Crunchyroll lacks full regional rights; "below average" dubbing; competition from JioCinema's Anime Hub (about 60 titles) and Airtel/Sony Yay's Anime Booth.
  - Purini: "We have essentially been in the market for two years."
  — [Rest of World, 15 Aug 2024](https://restofworld.org/2024/crunchyroll-india-subscription-price/)
- Crunchyroll said in 2023 it planned 20–30 shows per quarter, localisation and Indian offices. — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/crunchyrolls-ambitious-anime-plans-for-india). A second Indian office in Hyderabad and India-themed anime and merchandise were planned. — [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/crunchyroll-india-subscription-price/)
- Oct 2026: Crunchyroll launched digital manga in India (250+ English titles) inside an "Ultimate Fan" tier at ₹299/month or ₹999/year. It cited its own survey: 81% of Indian anime fans read digital manga at least monthly. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/crunchyroll-manga-launches-in-india-with-250-titles)
- India is called Crunchyroll's "second-largest market globally" (no data given; likely viewership). Crunchyroll's per-season investment is roughly US$100k–300k+ per 12-episode season (industry estimate). JioHotstar buys in bulk from regional distributor Muse Communication. — [Outlook Respawn, Sep/Oct 2025](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-guides/anime-licensing-in-india-how-your-favourite-shows-get-streamed) (weak on ranking)
- **Hakuhodo India "THE TIME IS NOW" (Jul 2026, 8 cities)**
  - India is the world's third-largest anime viewing market.
  - 61.1% prefer dubbed anime, and 73.1% of those prefer Hindi.
  - Merchandise spend is small: the modal six-month spend was ₹1,500–2,000 for working professionals (35.3%) and ₹500–1,000 for students and homemakers (36.8%). Only 2.1–2.9% spent ₹6,000+.
  - Recommends localised content, trusted distribution and lifestyle merchandise.
  — [MediaNews4U, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.medianews4u.com/?p=2512580)
- JetSynthesys survey (2024): 83% prefer anime over other animation, and 84% are willing to buy merchandise. The survey links this to a shortage of homegrown merchandise players. — [BusinessWorld](https://businessworld.in/index.php/article/83-indians-prefer-anime-over-animated-content-report--425846) (self-reported brand survey; weak)
- Market forecasts (weak, paid market-research):
  - Polaris projects India's anime market at over US$5B by 2032 (13.3% CAGR). — [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/crunchyroll-india-subscription-price/)
  - dentsu cites a Polaris forecast of about ¥860B for the Indian animation market by 2032. — [dentsu JP release PDF](https://www.group.dentsu.com/jp/news/release/pdf-cms/2024073-1211.pdf)

**Company statements naming India as a priority**
- **Toei Animation (One Piece, Dragon Ball)**: its 10-year global plan (reported Nov 2025) names India a primary focus alongside North America, China and Europe. It commits about ¥20B (about US$130M) over 5 years to overseas IP creation (25 new titles, including India) plus ¥19B for logistics, e-commerce, stores and events. Executives call India a "rapidly growing entertainment market." — [Outlook Respawn, 4 Nov 2025](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/toei-animation-targets-india-as-key-market-in-10-year-global-plan). Earlier (about 2022–23) Toei said it would open offices in India and Mexico after Italy. — [Anime Explained](https://www.animeexplained.com/news/toei-animation-to-open-offices-in-italy-india-and-mexico/)
- **Bandai Namco**: set up Bandai Namco India Pvt Ltd in June 2015 under Bandai Namco Asia, initially for amusement centres (Mumbai). — [Bandai Namco press release](https://www.bandainamco.co.jp/files/C-11-1320Open20a20directly20managed20amusement20fa.pdf). It is reportedly preparing made-in-India One Piece, Dragon Ball and Naruto apparel. It has relied on imported figures and Gunpla distributed through partners such as Fat Cat Collectibles and Wizplex. — [Outlook Respawn, May 2026](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/bandai-namco-made-in-india-anime-merchandise) (unnamed sources; unconfirmed). The India COO said in 2023 that apparel would be considered if demand justified it. — [AnimeHunch](https://animehunch.com/bandai-namco-india-says-we-are-going-to-provide-products-for-all-anime-fans/)
- **dentsu (2024)**: it had been rare for IP holders such as anime production companies and publishers to take part in Japanese-anime events in India. — [dentsu JP release PDF](https://www.group.dentsu.com/jp/news/release/pdf-cms/2024073-1211.pdf)

### Inferences
- Under-monetisation in one comparison: India is a top-3 viewing market, but the largest specialist platform's app had about 46k MAU (2024) against 15M paid subscribers globally, at about US$1/month pricing. Even generously assumed, India is likely a very small fraction of Crunchyroll revenue and of AJA's ¥2.17T overseas figure. This is an inference; no Indian revenue figure is public.
- Merchandise is the obvious gap. Fans say they want it (84% willing per JetSynthesys). Spend is low (₹500–2,000 per six months). Supply is import-led and goes through a few distributors. Even Bandai Namco is only now considering local manufacturing.

### Gaps
- I found no public figure for Japanese content or anime revenue from India or South Asia, from AJA, METI, JETRO or company IR.
- No Indian subscriber count for Crunchyroll has been disclosed.
- I found nothing India-specific for Aniplex, TMS, Toho, Shueisha or Kodansha in this search round.
- Sony Group IR statements on India were not retrieved; only Crunchyroll executives' press statements.

## 5. Japan–India cooperation on content and creative industries (JETRO, Cool Japan, MoUs, Year of Exchange)

### Takeaway
Cooperation has moved from a 2013 framework to concrete 2024–2027 activity:
- a JETRO content officer in New Delhi
- JETRO and JNTO-backed anime festivals in Delhi (about 47k attendees in 2024, about 60k in 2025)
- WAVES-linked M&E dialogues and an Indian call for a co-production MoU (2025)
- a JETRO report on India's animation industry (Jan 2026)
- the 2027 "India–Japan Year of Shared Horizons", with a dedicated "IMAGE-75: Manga, Anime, Gaming" partnership

I found no direct Cool Japan Fund investment in an Indian content company.

### Cited Findings
- **2013 ministerial joint statement** (India I&B Minister Manish Tewari and Japan METI Minister Toshimitsu Motegi): agreed to promote cooperation among the film and animation industries through film festivals, co-production and other collaboration, and set up a Joint Working Group. — [Embassy of Japan in India PDF](https://www.in.emb-japan.go.jp/PDF/Jt_st_tewari_motegi.pdf) (date per search summary; older data, 2013)
- **"IMAGE-75: Manga, Anime, Gaming" (announced by India's MEA, 2 Jul 2026)**: 2027 is designated the "India–Japan Year of Shared Horizons" (75 years of diplomatic ties), with India–Japan Week from 28 Apr 2027. IMAGE-75 is a partnership to support exchanges and co-creation in manga, anime and gaming. The stated rationale is that these are gaining popularity among Indian youth and that India is emerging as a major hub for animation, gaming, comics and immersive tech. — [NewKerala/IANS](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/2027-celebrated-as-india-japan-year-shared-horizons-314.htm); [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/world/india-japan-year-of-shared-horizons-to-celebrate-75-years-of-ties-articleshow-614lauy)
- **WAVES-linked dialogues (2025)**
  - March 2025: the Embassy of India in Tokyo held an M&E collaboration event ahead of the WAVES Summit in Mumbai. — [Embassy of India Tokyo](https://www.indembassy-tokyo.gov.in/eoityo_listview/MjI2Mg,,)
  - 18 Jun 2025: an event on India–Japan AVGC-XR partnership. JETRO's Takashi Tsuchiya spoke on anime's growing popularity in India and JETRO's content-export support. India's MoS for I&B advocated an MoU on content co-production and invited Japanese participation at IFFI 2025 and WAVES Film Bazaar. — [Embassy of India Tokyo press release PDF](https://www.indembassy-tokyo.gov.in/public_files/assets/pdf/Press_Release_WAVES_Event_18_June_2025.pdf)
- **JETRO report, 21 Jan 2026**: "Expanding India Entertainment Market (1): Current State and Outlook of the Animation Industry", by JETRO New Delhi with NRI India support.
  - It cites the Indian M&E market at about US$30B (2023), projected about US$100B by 2030 (IBEF).
  - It profiles Green Gold (Chhota Bheem; YouTube 22M+ subscribers; remaking Oboccharmaru with TV Asahi) and Reliance Animation (Little Singham; Sammy and Raj with Nickelodeon India).

Gojo is a Japan-based microfinance group with Indian subsidiaries, not a content company. — [Bar & Bench, Mar 2023](https://www.barandbench.com/dealstreet/jsa-acts-on-cool-japan-fund-investment-in-gojo-company-inc). The fund was launched in 2013 with an aim to invest about ¥90B in globalising Japanese businesses (media, food, fashion). — [AVCJ](https://www.avcj.com/avcj/news/3005354/government-backed-cool-japan-invests-500-startups-fund) (older data)

### Inferences
- The institutional trend runs one way, from occasional events (2013 framework, 2024 screening) to a named bilateral anime, manga and gaming partnership in 2027. A JETRO hackathon pitch can present a licensing or merchandise platform as infrastructure for IMAGE-75 and for the ¥20T export target.

## 6. Licensing practice barriers: minimum guarantees, approval timelines, small deals in emerging markets, master licensees and agents in India

### Takeaway
I found general evidence, mostly not India-specific:
- Anime licensing is built around minimum guarantees (MGs) sized for large territories (five to six figures USD per episode for new shows).
- Licensors prefer licensees with a track record.
- Production committees approve merchandise unanimously at monthly meetings, which slows product launches even in the US.
- In India, rights are fragmented (e.g., Naruto unavailable on Crunchyroll) and go through regional distributors (Muse Communication) and import partners.

Together these support the claim that small Indian merchandise or licensing deals are structurally hard to do. Direct data on India MGs or approval times was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Deal structures (ANN, 2021; older data)**
  - The most common structure for new shows is an MG plus royalties. Licensors want an MG large enough that the deal is "worth it."
  - Indicative per-episode MGs: A+ simulcast (North America) up to US$250k, sometimes US$400k; B/B+ new show US$70k–150k; C-tier simulcast low five figures; non-exclusive catalog a couple of thousand dollars or under US$1,000.
  - Revenue-share deals without an MG are more common for catalog titles, smaller non-English markets and non-exclusive deals.
  - Licences are sold in multi-territory blocks (e.g., "World Except Asia"). Bids for new shows can be due a year or more before production. Foreign companies are rarely invited onto production committees. Licensors favour "a well-established company with a history of paying on time."
  — [Anime News Network, 2 Aug 2021](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2021-08-02/how-much-does-it-cost-to-license-anime-series/.175579)
- Streaming royalties are often 15–30% of net revenue, paid only once they exceed the MG floor. — [Vitrina guide](https://vitrina.ai/?p=141418) (marketing vendor; weak)
- **Approval bottleneck (merchandise)**: the production committee (broadcasters, publishers, toy makers, studios) must approve each new product unanimously and meets once a month. Hot Topic plans about 100 product types for a new anime, but often only a few arrive in time. Marubeni and Shogakukan's JV Mag.net is testing AI to speed product reviews. Trading houses have "more than a dozen" talks under way in Asia and South America. — [Briefs Finance, 1 Jul 2026, citing Bloomberg](https://www.briefs.co/news/japan-s-big-trading-firms-target-24-billion-anime-goods-mark/)
- Complex rights-holder arrangements in Japan, slow international releases and fan-subtitled piracy have historically limited anime's financial returns. — [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/japanese-anime-making-money-807789/) (older and undated in results)
- **India-specific rights fragmentation and intermediaries**
  - Crunchyroll lacks full regional rights for some titles; Naruto was unavailable in India. — [Rest of World, 2024](https://restofworld.org/2024/crunchyroll-india-subscription-price/)
  - JioHotstar buys anime in bulk from regional distributor Muse Communication. Crunchyroll invests in Japanese committees (Aniplex, Kadokawa). — [Outlook Respawn, 2025](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-guides/anime-licensing-in-india-how-your-favourite-shows-get-streamed)
  - Bandai Namco India relies on imported product distributed through partners (Fat Cat Collectibles, Wizplex). — [Outlook Respawn, May 2026](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/bandai-namco-made-in-india-anime-merchandise)
- JETRO lists Indian market barriers for Japanese animation: strict children's-TV rules, localisation, Hindi and regional dubbing quality, rampant YouTube piracy, and the urban OTT vs rural cable split. — [JETRO, Jan 2026](https://www.jetro.go.jp/biz/areareports/2026/f5f42c2bc162035c.html)
- **India licensed-merchandise market size (older, conflicting, 2016 data)**: LIMA-based figures put India about #20 globally with US$1.26–1.40B in licensed retail sales in 2016. The Licensing Letter put it at US$811M. — [The Licensing Letter](https://www.thelicensingletter.com/?p=1396); [India Retailing](https://www.indiaretailing.com/?p=107322) (old, conflicting; use only as an order of magnitude)

### Inferences
- MGs are sized for North American simulcasts, and committees require unanimous approval at monthly meetings. A licensor's fixed cost per deal (review, approvals, compliance) is broadly the same for a ₹10 lakh Indian apparel deal as for a US deal. Small emerging-market deals are therefore unattractive unless an aggregator or master licensee bundles them, or approvals become cheaper (e.g., the AI review trials). This is inferred from the cited findings, not stated by a source.
- Indian supply of Japanese anime IP runs through a few intermediaries (Muse for video, a few import distributors for merchandise), with rights gaps on major titles. This is consistent with Japanese licensors treating India as a low-priority add-on territory. 2025–26 statements from Toei, Bandai Namco and Crunchyroll suggest that is changing.

### Gaps
- I found no source on typical MG sizes or royalty rates for merchandise licences of Japanese anime IP in India.
- I found no source on approval timelines specific to Indian licensees.
- I found no evidence on "Dream Theatre" or other named Indian master licensees or agents for Japanese anime merchandise. Not verified; do not cite.
- I found no direct quote from a Japanese licensor explaining why it avoids small emerging-market deals; that argument is inferred.
- The current (2025) India figure from Licensing International's study is paywalled and was not retrieved.
- A MIXI US$50M CVC fund for Indian gaming and digital-entertainment startups (Aug 2023) appeared in a search snippet without a confirmed source URL. Treat it as unverified.
